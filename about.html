MOBILITY TALK · ABOUT

About this site.

Who runs Mobility Talk, where the information comes from, what happens to what you type, and how to tell us when something is wrong or hard to use.

What this is

Mobility Talk is a free directory and guide for people with disabilities, their families and the people who help them. It began with wheelchairs, ramps and equipment repair, and now covers disability more broadly: benefits and health coverage, housing, rides, in-home help, work and school, vision and hearing services, rights and crisis lines.

It lists programs for all 50 states and the District of Columbia, and it has begun adding other countries, starting with the national programs, crisis lines and emergency numbers for twelve of them on the worldwide page. Listings for the U.S. territories have started and are still few.

Who runs it

Mobility Talk is an independent site created and maintained by Travis Vought. He serves as Finance Officer of the Mobility Independence Foundation, which is featured on the site. Gifts and sponsorships made through the partner page are received by Veteran Home Guardians, an Ohio 501(c)(3) nonprofit.

There is no paid placement. Sponsors are thanked on the partner page and are never mixed into program results. Any advertising is labeled and kept apart from the directory (see the partner page).

Where the information comes from

  • Each listing links to the page it came from: the program’s own page, a government page or a national directory. The link is labeled so you can tell which.
  • Each listing shows the date its source page was checked.
  • A phone number is shown only when the source page showed it.
  • “Listing: confirm details” means the entry came from a directory or a news report and not from the program’s own page.
  • The “Before you call” lists are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules.

This site gives general information. It is not legal, medical or tax advice, and it does not decide whether you qualify for a program.

Report a problem

Programs change. If a number is wrong, a program has closed or one is missing, use the “Report a problem” link on any listing or email travis@vethomeguard.org. Corrections are checked against the program’s own page.

Privacy

  • There is no sign-in.
  • Nothing you type or choose on this site is saved or sent anywhere. Message drafts and notes stay in your open page until you close it.
  • The maps on the home page and the local help page show pictures from OpenStreetMap, which sees which area you look at. “Use my location” stays on your device. More about the maps.
  • Links you copy to share carry only choices such as the state and the kind of help, never your own words.
  • Phone, email and website links go to other organizations, which have their own privacy practices.
  • A visit counter, GoatCounter, counts page views without cookies. It records the page, the site you came from, your browser and screen size, and an approximate country; it does not store your IP address.
  • The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which may keep server logs, including IP addresses, for security.
  • The site shows Google ads, non-personalized only, on the guides and the state and country pages, never on the help directory. Google’s ad code can set its own cookies. Any ad sold directly is labeled “Advertisement” and kept apart from the directory.

The privacy page has the details.

Accessibility

People with disabilities are who this site is for, so it has to work for them.

What has been done

  • Every page type is checked with an automated accessibility checker (axe-core) at desktop and phone widths before changes are published.
  • Pages can be used with a keyboard alone. There is a skip link at the top of each page, and the item that has focus is outlined.
  • Every state has a plain page that works without JavaScript, and a help list you can print.
  • Phone numbers come first on listings because many people do not have internet at home. If you are deaf, hard of hearing or have a speech disability, you can dial 711 to place a relay call to any number listed.
  • Links that open another website in a new tab say so.

What has not been done yet

  • The site has not had a formal accessibility audit, and it has not yet been tested with people who use screen readers, switches, voice control or screen magnifiers. Automated checks cannot find every barrier.
  • The maps are a shortcut. The map itself is a picture that is hard to use with a screen reader or a keyboard, so every state or county on it is also a button in the list beside it, with the same count, and everything the map does can also be done with the state and County menus. “Use my location” has a menu to fall back on too.
  • The site is in English, with one page of ayuda en español. The Spanish on that page has not yet been reviewed by a fluent speaker.
  • Other organizations’ websites that we link to may not be accessible.

Tell us about a barrier

If something on this site is hard or impossible for you to use, email travis@vethomeguard.org. Say what page you were on and what you were trying to do. We will fix it, and we can send you the information another way.
