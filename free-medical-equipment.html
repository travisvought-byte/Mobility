Borrow it or get it used: loan closets and reuse

Every state has a public assistive technology program that lends equipment and passes on equipment others no longer need. [1]

Where to look, in order

  1. Call your state’s assistive technology (AT) program. Ask about a short-term loan while you wait, and about reused equipment you can keep. [1]
  2. Search GotDME for programs near you that lend or donate medical equipment. [2]
  3. Click your state in the national AT reuse database to find other reuse programs nearby. [3]
  • Every state, D.C., Puerto Rico and the U.S. territories has a federally funded AT program. [1]
  • AT programs run short-term device loans, so you can borrow equipment for a limited time and try it before you buy. [1]
  • They run reuse programs that pass equipment the first owner no longer needs to a new owner, at substantial savings. [1]
  • They hold demonstrations where you can compare devices with a professional’s help, and they help people buy equipment through financial loans and other means. [1]

Medicare: equipment and repairs

Medicare Part B covers medically necessary durable medical equipment, such as wheelchairs, scooters, walkers and hospital beds. [4]

  • Your doctor or other health care provider must prescribe it for use in your home. [4]
  • After you meet the Part B deductible, you pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount, if your supplier accepts assignment. [4]
  • Use doctors and suppliers enrolled in Medicare, and ask before you get the equipment whether the supplier accepts assignment. One that does not may charge more and have you pay the full cost up front until Medicare pays you back. [4]
  • Depending on the item, you may have to rent it, have to buy it, or be able to choose. Some items become yours after a set number of rental payments. [4]
  • If you own the equipment, Medicare pays 80% of the Medicare-approved amount for repairs, up to the cost of replacing the item, and you pay the other 20%. The supplier that sold it to you does not have to repair it. [5]
  • If you rent it, repairs and replacement parts are the supplier’s responsibility. [5]

Medicaid: equipment, and items not on the list

Under federal Medicaid rules, home health services include medical supplies, equipment and appliances suitable for use anywhere normal life activities take place. [6]

  • States may keep a list of preapproved equipment for ease, but they may not have absolute exclusions of coverage for medical equipment, supplies or appliances. [6]
  • Each state must have a process, with criteria, for asking for an item that is not on its list. [6]
  • Medicaid’s coverage of equipment is not limited to the items Medicare covers as durable medical equipment. [6]

If Medicaid says no because an item is not on its list, ask your state Medicaid agency how to request it as an exception, and keep the denial letter. Your state’s Medicaid agency is on your state’s page, and Know your rights covers appealing a benefit denial.

Veterans: wheelchairs and equipment through VA

VA provides prosthetic items and equipment to veterans enrolled in VA health care who have a medical need for them. [7]

  • Items include durable medical equipment and mobility aids, communication and assistive devices, home oxygen, aids for blindness and low vision, and repairs to custom orthotic and prosthetic devices. [7]
  • Wheelchairs are among the items VA’s Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service handles. For your own eligibility, VA says to contact the Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service near you, which you can find with the VA facility locator. [8]

Equipment grants

Some national groups for a condition help their members pay for equipment. [9]

  • The Muscular Dystrophy Association’s equipment grant gives up to $1,000 per calendar year to an MDA member living with a neuromuscular disease, toward items such as manual and power wheelchairs, scooters, lift chairs and batteries. Income does not affect eligibility. [9]
  • Apply before you order: MDA cannot pay back items already bought or ordered. You need a quote or unpaid invoice, the vendor’s W-9 and a letter of medical necessity from your health care provider. [9]

Quick answers

Where can I borrow a wheelchair for free?

Start with your state’s assistive technology program, which lends equipment for a limited time and passes on used equipment. GotDME and the national AT reuse database list other programs that lend or donate equipment. [1][2][3]

Does Medicare pay for a wheelchair?

Medicare Part B covers medically necessary durable medical equipment, including wheelchairs and scooters, when your doctor or other provider prescribes it for use in your home. After the Part B deductible, you pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount if the supplier accepts assignment. [4]

Does Medicare pay to repair a wheelchair I own?

Yes. Medicare pays 80% of the Medicare-approved amount for repairs, up to the cost of replacing the item, and you pay 20%. The supplier that sold it to you does not have to do the repair. For rented equipment, repairs are the supplier’s responsibility. [5]

Medicaid says the item is not on its list. Can I still get it?

You can ask. Federal rules let states keep a list of preapproved equipment, but not absolute exclusions, and each state must have a process for requesting items that are not on its list. [6]

Can veterans get a wheelchair from the VA?

VA provides equipment, including mobility aids, to veterans enrolled in VA health care who have a medical need for it. Contact the Prosthetic and Sensory Aids Service at your local VA for eligibility. [7][8]

Are there grants for wheelchairs?

Some condition groups offer them. The Muscular Dystrophy Association gives MDA members with neuromuscular diseases up to $1,000 a calendar year toward items such as wheelchairs, scooters and lift chairs. Apply before you order. [9]
