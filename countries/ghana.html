Disability resources in Ghana

Mobility Talk began in the United States and is now adding other countries. These are the first listings for Ghana: 4 national programs and lines, each checked against its own page or an official directory (most recently on 2026-11-21). Local and regional services are not listed yet.

In danger or in crisis right now?

In Ghana, call 112 (Police, fire and ambulance).

National Mental Health and Suicide Prevention Helpline: 0800 678 678.

Numbers are written the way they are dialed inside Ghana. Calling from another country? Use the country code, and note that some free numbers only work inside the country.

Crisis and someone to talk to

National Mental Health and Suicide Prevention Helpline

Mental Health Authority

A free 24-hour line from Ghana's Mental Health Authority, where you can talk to a counsellor about any mental health worry, including thoughts of suicide.

Next step: Call 0800 678 678. It is toll-free.

Where to start

National Council on Persons with Disability (NCPD)

Ministry of Gender, Children and Social Protection

The government body set up under the Persons with Disability Act, 2006 (Act 715) to shape disability policy. It keeps the national register of persons with disability and of the organisations that serve them.

Next step: Contact the Ministry, which the Council sits under, to ask about registering.

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

Commission on Human Rights and Administrative Justice (CHRAJ)

CHRAJ

Takes complaints about human rights violations, including discrimination, free of charge. Complain within 12 months of what happened.

Next step: Call the toll-free line, email info@chraj.gov.gh, or visit any CHRAJ office, weekdays 8am to 6pm. To report discrimination from any phone without data or credit, dial *920*59#; a case officer calls back within 48 hours.

Ghana Federation of Disability Organisations (GFD)

Ghana Federation of Disability Organisations

The national umbrella of organisations of persons with disabilities, set up in 1987. It advocates for disability rights and helps its member organisations grow.

Next step: Call its toll-free number.
