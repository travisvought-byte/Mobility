Disability resources in Ireland

In danger or in crisis right now?

In Ireland, call 112 (Police, fire and ambulance (999 works too). Free from any phone).

Samaritans: 116 123.

Numbers are written the way they are dialed inside Ireland. Calling from another country? Use the country code, and note that some free numbers only work inside the country.

Crisis and someone to talk to

Samaritans

Samaritans Ireland

Someone to talk to, free, any time of day or night, whatever you are going through. You do not have to be suicidal to call.

Next step: Call 116 123 from any phone.

Where to start

Citizens Information Phone Service

Citizens Information Board

Free, confidential information on public services and entitlements, including disability payments, grants and services. Local Citizens Information Centres can also help you fill in forms.

Next step: Call Monday to Friday, 9am to 8pm. Calls cost the same as a landline call. From outside Ireland, call +353 21 229 8178.

Disability payments and benefits

Disability Allowance

Department of Social Protection

A weekly payment for people aged 16 to 65 with a disability expected to last at least a year that substantially limits the work they can do. It is means tested, and you must live in Ireland. You can keep attending school while you get it.

Next step: Fill in the application form on the gov.ie page, or get one at your Intreo centre or Citizens Information Centre, and send it with your supporting documents to the Disability Allowance Section.

Equipment, home changes and getting around

Housing Adaptation Grant for Disabled People

Your local authority (county or city council)

A means-tested grant toward changes that make a private home suitable for a disabled person, such as access and moving around inside. The maximum grant is €40,000. Social housing tenants use a separate scheme run by their council.

Next step: Get the application form from your local authority's housing grants page or office, have your doctor complete the medical section, and return it to the council.

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

National Advocacy Service for People with Disabilities

Citizens Information Board

An independent, confidential and free advocacy service. An advocate can speak up for you or help you be heard when dealing with services.

Next step: Call or email info@advocacy.ie. For discrimination at work or in services, IHREC's Your Rights pages explain the law; complaints go to the Workplace Relations Commission.
