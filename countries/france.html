FRANCE · DISABILITY RESOURCES

Disability resources in France

Mobility Talk began in the United States and is now adding other countries. These are the first listings for France: 6 national programs and lines, each checked against its own page or an official directory (most recently on 2026-11-21). Local and regional services are not listed yet.

In danger or in crisis right now?

In France, call 112 (European emergency number. In France, 15 is medical emergencies (SAMU), 17 the police and 18 the fire brigade).

3114: national suicide prevention line: 3114.

Numbers are written the way they are dialed inside France. Calling from another country? Use the country code, and note that some free numbers only work inside the country.

Calling without your voice? Deaf, hard of hearing, deafblind or aphasic: contact 114 by text message, video, chat or the urgence 114 app. It is free and open at any hour.

Crisis and someone to talk to

3114: national suicide prevention line

Numéro national de prévention du suicide

A free line open around the clock anywhere in France, for anyone thinking about suicide and for worried family and friends. Nurses and psychologists trained in crisis care answer, and they can send emergency help with the SAMU when needed.

Next step: Call 3114. The line is in French.

Where to start

MDPH: departmental disability office

Maison départementale des personnes handicapées

Each département has an MDPH, the single place to get information and apply for disability help. Its commission (CDAPH) decides on the AAH allowance, the disability compensation benefit (PCH), the mobility inclusion card and recognition as a disabled worker (RQTH). The MDPH for the département where you have lived for more than three months handles your file.

Next step: Find your MDPH by postcode in the directory on Mon Parcours Handicap. Many accept applications online. Services are in French.

Disability payments and benefits

Allocation aux adultes handicapés (AAH)

Decided by the MDPH; paid by the CAF or MSA

A monthly allowance for disabled adults with low incomes. You need an incapacity rate of at least 80%, or 50% to 79% with a substantial and lasting barrier to finding work, and you usually need to be 20 or over. The maximum was €1,041.59 a month in mainland France when this was checked.

Next step: Apply to your MDPH, online where it offers that or by post on the official form. The commission has four months to answer; no answer by then counts as a refusal. In French.

Equipment, home changes and getting around

Mobility inclusion card for parking (CMI stationnement)

Lets you park free and with no time limit in public on-street spaces, and use disabled parking spaces, as driver or passenger. It is for people whose walking is very limited (for example, under 200 metres) or who need someone with them to get around because of a mental, cognitive, psychiatric or visual impairment, lasting at least a year. The card is free.

Next step: Under 60, apply to your MDPH on form Cerfa 15692 with ID, proof of address and a medical certificate. At 60 or over, ask for it in your APA application. The old parking card stays valid until 31 December 2026. In French.

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

Défenseur des droits

The independent rights authority defends people free of charge when their rights are not respected, including disability discrimination at work, in housing and at school. Disability is one of the most common reasons people turn to it.

Next step: Call Monday to Friday, 8:30 to 19:30, use the online form, or book a free meeting with one of its local delegates. For discrimination, its platform also answers on 3928 (local rate, weekdays 9:30 to 19:00) and by chat; deaf people can use the Acceo service. In French.
