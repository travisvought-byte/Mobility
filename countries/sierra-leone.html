Disability resources in Sierra Leone

Living with a disability in Sierra Leone, or caring for someone who is? Here is what is listed for Sierra Leone, checked against official pages:

  • First step: National Commission for Persons with Disability (NCPD)
  • If you are treated unfairly: Human Rights Commission of Sierra Leone (HRCSL)

Where to start

National Commission for Persons with Disability (NCPD)

Government of Sierra Leone

The government body set up under the Persons with Disability Act 2011 to coordinate disability policy and keep a register and database of persons with disabilities; it has regional offices. Under the Act, persons with disabilities are entitled to free medical services in public health institutions and free tertiary education. A 2026 UNICEF budget brief says free health care has not yet been fully delivered in practice.

Next step: Ask the NCPD's regional office about registering. Complaints of disability discrimination can go to the Human Rights Commission's Disability Unit.

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

Human Rights Commission of Sierra Leone (HRCSL)

Human Rights Commission of Sierra Leone

Sierra Leone's national human rights institution, set up in 2004; its remit includes protecting the rights of persons with disabilities, and in 2024 it agreed a complaints-handling arrangement with the National Commission for Persons with Disability.

Next step: Contact the Commission in Freetown by phone, or through its website.

Crisis and someone to talk to

116 GBV Hotline

Ministry of Gender and Children's Affairs

A toll-free hotline set up by the Ministry of Gender and Children's Affairs, with UNFPA and other partners, offering counselling and referral to survivors of gender-based violence. The government still named it among its efforts in July 2025. It is a violence line, not a general mental health line, and its hours are not stated.

Next step: Call 116. In an emergency, call 117.
