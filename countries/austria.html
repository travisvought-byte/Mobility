AUSTRIA · DISABILITY RESOURCES

Disability resources in Austria

Living with a disability in Austria, or caring for someone who is? Here is what is listed for Austria, checked against official pages:

  • First step: Disability pass (Behindertenpass)
  • Payments: Care allowance (Pflegegeld)
  • Equipment and getting around: Disabled parking card (Parkausweis nach § 29b StVO)
  • If you are treated unfairly: Federal Disability Ombudsperson (Behindertenanwältin)

Where to start

Disability pass (Behindertenpass)

Sozialministeriumservice (SMS)

An official photo ID that proves a degree of disability of at least 50%. It brings discounts, and from 70% a half-price fare on Austrian Federal Railways (ÖBB). It is also the first step to the disabled parking card. It pays no money itself.

Next step: Apply to the regional office of the Sozialministeriumservice with a photo and your medical records or earlier decisions. If you have no assessment yet, an SMS doctor estimates your degree of disability, usually from your documents. In German.

Disability payments and benefits

Care allowance (Pflegegeld)

Austrian federal care allowance

A monthly allowance, in seven levels, for people who need more than 65 hours of care and support a month because of a physical, mental, psychological or sensory disability expected to last at least six months, whatever the cause. You must normally live in Austria.

Next step: The responsible body sets your level after an expert assessment, usually an announced home visit by a doctor or nurse. The government’s help portal explains where to apply. In German.

Equipment, home changes and getting around

Disabled parking card (Parkausweis nach § 29b StVO)

Sozialministeriumservice (SMS)

A parking card for people whose disability pass carries the entry that they cannot use public transport because of a permanent loss of mobility. In most provinces, cardholders park free.

Next step: Apply to the Sozialministeriumservice on its digital or paper form with a passport photo. The government portal has the details in English.

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

Federal Disability Ombudsperson (Behindertenanwältin)

Anwaltschaft für Gleichbehandlungsfragen für Menschen mit Behinderungen

Free advice, support and representation if you feel you have been discriminated against because of a disability under federal equality or employment law. It can also run conciliation proceedings for you at the Sozialministeriumservice.

Next step: Call free on 0800 80 80 16, every day from 9:00 to 12:00, email office@behindertenanwalt.gv.at, or come to a consultation day in your province. In German.

Crisis and someone to talk to

TelefonSeelsorge (Notruf 142)

TelefonSeelsorge

A free listening line open every day around the clock, with email and chat counselling too.

Next step: Call 142, any hour; there is no charge from any phone in Austria. In German.
