Crisis and someone to talk to

TelefonSeelsorge

A free, anonymous listening line for anyone in crisis or emotional distress, open around the clock. You can also write by email or chat.

Next step: Call 0800 111 0 111 or 0800 111 0 222, or the European number 116 123. The service is in German.

Where to start

Citizens’ line for people with disabilities (Bürgertelefon)

Federal Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs (BMAS)

The federal labour ministry’s information line for questions about disability. Deaf people can reach the ministry in German Sign Language through its sign-language video phone, or by email at info.gehoerlos@bmas.bund.de.

Next step: Call Monday to Thursday, 8:00 to 17:00, or Friday, 8:00 to 12:00. The line is in German.

Independent participation counselling (EUTB)

Ergänzende unabhängige Teilhabeberatung, funded by BMAS

Free counselling centres across Germany for disabled people, people at risk of disability and their families. Advisers, some of them disabled, help with money, living independently, wheelchairs and other aids, interpreters and taking part in community life. The centres are not run by service providers, and they do not take on court cases.

Next step: Find the centre nearest you on teilhabeberatung.de. Counselling is in German; the ministry publishes a brochure about it in English.

Severe disability ID card (Schwerbehindertenausweis)

Versorgungsamt (disability assessment office) where you live

The office assesses your degree of disability (Grad der Behinderung, GdB). With a GdB of at least 50 you count as severely disabled and get the ID card. Depending on the conditions, it can mean free local public transport or lower vehicle tax.

Next step: Apply to the Versorgungsamt for where you live, on its form or online where offered. You sign a release so the office can request your medical records, which costs you nothing. Offices and forms differ by state, and the process is in German.

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

Conciliation Service under the Disability Equality Act (Schlichtungsstelle BGG)

Office of the Federal Government Commissioner for Matters relating to Persons with Disabilities

Free out-of-court conciliation when a federal public body fails on accessibility or discriminates because of a disability, or when you, or you with your service dog, are refused entry to a place open to the public. You do not need a lawyer or proof of your degree of disability. It does not cover state or local authorities, private shops’ accessibility or private-sector job disputes.

Next step: Apply in writing, through the contact form, or in person by appointment (Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 12:00). Applications by phone are not possible. Its website has pages in English.
