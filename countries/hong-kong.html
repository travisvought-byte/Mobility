HONG KONG · DISABILITY RESOURCES

Disability resources in Hong Kong

Living with a disability in Hong Kong, or caring for someone who is? Here is what is listed for Hong Kong, checked against official pages:

  • First step: Social Welfare Department hotline
  • If you are treated unfairly: Equal Opportunities Commission (EOC)

Where to start

Social Welfare Department hotline

Social Welfare Department

The 24-hour hotline of the department that runs rehabilitation services. It gives recorded welfare information, connects you to SWD offices and social security enquiries, and puts you through to a social worker for a family crisis or emotional distress.

Next step: Call 2343 2255. Choose a language, then press 0 for a social worker (Monday to Friday 9:00 to 17:00, Saturday 9:00 to 12:00; at other times the call goes to an outreach team run by Tung Wah Group of Hospitals). For day training or residential rehabilitation services, a school, medical or family social worker applies for you through the Central Referral System.

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

Equal Opportunities Commission (EOC)

Equal Opportunities Commission, Hong Kong

Takes complaints of disability discrimination under the Disability Discrimination Ordinance; the hotline or email can help you prepare a written complaint.

Next step: Lodge a written complaint by the EOC online form, fax, post or in person, or call the enquiry hotline for help.

Crisis and someone to talk to

18111 Mental Health Support Hotline

Health Bureau

A government-funded hotline answered 24 hours a day, all year, giving immediate support and counselling and referring callers to the most suitable service. Since November 2025 it also offers WhatsApp messaging and scheduled call-backs.

Next step: Call 18111, at any hour. Help is available in Cantonese, Mandarin and English, plus Hindi, Nepali, Urdu, Punjabi, Bahasa Indonesia, Tagalog, Vietnamese, Bengali and Thai.

In danger or in crisis right now?

In Hong Kong, call 999 (Emergency. People with a speech or hearing impairment who have registered can send an SMS to 992; registration is free, online or at a police station).
