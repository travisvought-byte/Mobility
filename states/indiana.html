Ramps & home modification

Statewide

RAMP UP Indiana (IHCDA grants to ramp builders)

Indiana Housing and Community Development Authority

State program that gives grants to nonprofits to install exterior ramps for low-income homeowners at or below 80% of area median income. It funds ramp builders, not individuals.

Next step: Ask a local ramp nonprofit or Area Agency on Aging whether it receives RAMP UP funds; questions go to rampup@ihcda.in.gov.

Good to know
  • Homeowners at or below 80% of area median income.
  • Applications are by nonprofits; check that the program is open.

Servants at Work (SAWs): free wheelchair ramps

Servants at Work, Inc.

Faith-based volunteer nonprofit that builds ADA-compliant wooden wheelchair ramps for people with permanent or long-term disabilities in low-income households. It covers 70 of Indiana's 92 counties and partners with other ramp builders elsewhere.

Next step: Apply through the SAWs website or call to ask about your county.

Good to know
  • Low-income households; check your county is covered.

Relay Indiana equipment program: phone equipment for hearing loss

Relay Indiana equipment program

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as household income not above $74,000. Equipment is loaned.

Next step: Open the website, check the current eligibility and application steps, and ask a hearing health professional to certify your hearing loss if the program requires it.

Good to know

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Indiana Department of Transportation: rural public transit program (Section 5311)

Indiana Department of Transportation

The Indiana Department of Transportation runs the federal Section 5311 program, which funds public transit in towns and rural areas with fewer than 50,000 people. Rural transit systems in this state are paid for in part through this office, and many offer rides that people with disabilities can use.

Next step: Ask the state transit office, or look on the page, for the public transit provider that serves your county, then call that provider and ask about door-to-door rides and how to register.

Good to know
  • This is a state grants office, not a ride booking line.
  • Rural transit hours, fares and service areas are set by each local provider.
Official page

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (IN)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says Indiana uses a statewide broker, Verida, plus brokers hired by managed care organizations (information collected Sep 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.
Official page

How to reach them: Call the member services number printed on your Medicaid card.

Statewide

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (IN)

Indiana Department of Transportation

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. The state passes the money to eligible nonprofits, public agencies and transit operators, which run the vehicles and rides.

Next step: If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging, Center for Independent Living or local transit agency which 5310-funded rides serve your county. If you run a nonprofit or transit service, use this page to see how and when to apply.

Good to know
  • Funds go to eligible organizations, so riders use the services those groups run rather than applying for money themselves.
  • Application rounds, deadlines and forms change; check the page for the current cycle.

Indiana Medicaid home and community-based services screening

FSSA / DDARS / Office of Medicaid Policy and Planning

Referral to screening for Medicaid services that may support care at home, including PathWays for Aging and disability waiver programs.

Next step: For a new inquiry, ask your county aging agency which screening route fits. If already in PathWays, ask your health plan’s service coordinator about assessment and authorized services. For disability waivers, ask about the Health and Wellness, TBI, Family Supports or Community Integration and Habilitation route.

Good to know
  • Different programs have different age, disability, financial and care-assessment requirements. Waiver waiting lists may apply; general PathWays enrollment and its HCBS waiver are distinct.

Indiana Vocational Rehabilitation (Division of Disability and Rehabilitative Services)

The state vocational rehabilitation agency helps people with disabilities prepare for, find and keep work. When equipment is needed for a work goal it can pay for assistive technology and vehicle modifications, and in some states home modifications. Eligibility and the order of service vary by state.

Next step: Call to apply, and ask about assistive technology, vehicle modification and home modification funding before you buy anything.

Good to know
  • Services must connect to an employment goal.
  • Apply and get approval before buying equipment or starting modifications.
  • Some states keep a waitlist or order of selection.

Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Indiana)

USDA Rural Development, Indianapolis state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Indiana Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.
Call (317) 290-3100Official page

State office main line from the USDA Rural Development state office directory; confirm before calling

Statewide

iCanConnect in Indiana: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), Indiana program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including Indiana. The Indiana page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the Indiana page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.

Indiana Talking Book & Braille Library: talking-book and braille library service

Indiana Talking Book & Braille Library

Indiana Talking Book & Braille Library in Indianapolis is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving Indiana. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 317-232-3684 (in-state toll-free 800-622-4970), say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

Good to know
  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.

Indiana Institute on Disability and Community

Indiana University

Indiana Institute on Disability and Community is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at Indiana University. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

Next step: Call the center and ask what community projects, assistive technology help, or referrals it offers for adults and children with developmental disabilities in your area.

Good to know
  • Services vary by center and funding; many UCEDDs focus on training, research and referral rather than ongoing direct care.

Indiana Department of Health, Division of Trauma and Injury Prevention

Indiana Brain Injury Resource Facilitation is run by or through Indiana Department of Health, Division of Trauma and Injury Prevention, which the National Association of State Head Injury Administrators lists as the state lead on brain injury. Statewide

Great Lakes ADA Center

University of Illinois Chicago, Institute on Disability and Human Development

The Great Lakes ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by University of Illinois Chicago, Institute on Disability and Human Development.

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Statewide

Indiana State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Indiana Family and Social Services Administration, Office of General Counsel

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.
