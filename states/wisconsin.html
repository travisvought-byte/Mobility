Ramps & home modification

Programs that build, lend or pay for ramps, bathroom changes and other home access work.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Whether you own or rent. If you rent, ask the landlord in writing before any work.
  • Photos of the entrance and a count of the steps, or the height from the ground to the door.
  • What the person uses to get around: wheelchair, scooter, walker or cane.
  • Household size and income, if the program is based on income.

Ask

  • Does the work have to be approved before it starts?
  • Is this a grant, a loan or a free build by volunteers?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Equipment, repair & assistive technology

Try, borrow, repair, finance or reuse wheelchairs and assistive technology.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • The make, model and serial number of the equipment, if you have them.
  • Who paid for it and when: Medicare, Medicaid, the VA, private insurance or you.
  • The name of the supplier or dealer, and any warranty papers.
  • What is wrong, when it started and what it keeps you from doing.

Ask

  • Can I borrow or try equipment while I wait?
  • Is there a cost, and can it be reduced or waived?
  • How long is the wait, and who do I call to check on it?
  • Do you serve my county?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Rides & transportation

Rides and transportation for older adults and people with disabilities: state transit funding offices, paratransit, Medicaid rides and travel help.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • The addresses you are leaving from and going to, and the days and times you need.
  • Whether you use a wheelchair or scooter and need a lift or ramp.
  • For Medicaid rides: your Medicaid card and the appointment details.
  • Whether someone travels with you.

Ask

  • How far ahead do I have to book?
  • What does a ride cost, and is there a reduced fare?
  • How do I apply for ADA paratransit, and how long does approval take?
  • What do I do if the ride does not come?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Wisconsin Department of Transportation

The Wisconsin Department of Transportation runs the federal Section 5311 program, which funds public transit in towns and rural areas with fewer than 50,000 people. Income, benefits & health coverage

Social Security disability, Medicaid, Medicare counseling, ABLE savings accounts and help appealing a denial.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Your Social Security number and a photo ID.
  • Names, addresses and dates for your doctors, clinics and hospitals, and a list of your medicines.
  • Recent pay stubs, benefit letters and bank statements.
  • Any denial or decision letter. Note the date on it, because appeal deadlines are counted from the day you receive it.

Ask

  • Which program fits my situation, and can I apply for more than one?
  • What is the deadline to appeal, and where do I send the appeal?
  • Will working or saving money change my benefits?
  • Who can help me fill out the forms at no cost?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Wisconsin)

USDA Rural Development, Stevens Point state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Wisconsin Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.

Work, school & training

Vocational rehabilitation, job accommodations, discrimination charges and help for families with school plans.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • What kind of work or training you want, even if you are not sure yet.
  • Records that describe your disability, if you have them.
  • For a job problem: dates, names and copies of emails or letters.
  • For school: your child’s IEP or 504 plan and recent evaluations.

Ask

  • How do I apply, and how long until I am assigned a counselor?
  • Can you pay for equipment, training or changes to a vehicle that I need for work?
  • What is the deadline to file a complaint?
  • If I disagree with a decision, who can help me appeal?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Vision, hearing & communication

Services for people who are blind, have low vision, are deaf, hard of hearing or DeafBlind: training, reading services, phone equipment and relay.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • A recent eye exam or hearing test, if you have one.
  • What has become hard to do: reading mail, using the phone, getting around, working.
  • Proof of where you live and of income, if the program asks.
  • How you want to be contacted: voice, text, email, videophone or relay.

Ask

  • Do you come to my home, or do I travel to you?
  • Is the equipment free, loaned or sold?
  • Who can sign the form that confirms my vision or hearing loss?
  • Is there training for my family too?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Have ready

Ask

  • What is my deadline to complain or appeal?
  • Can you represent me, or only advise me?
  • What should I put in writing right now?

Wisconsin Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. Developmental disability agencies

County boards, regional centers and state offices for developmental disabilities. They decide eligibility, coordinate services and manage waiver funding.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Records that show when the disability began, such as school or medical evaluations.
  • Proof of where you live.
  • A Medicaid card, if you have one.

Ask

  • How do I apply for eligibility, and how long does it take?
  • Is there a waiting list for waiver services?
  • Who will be my service coordinator?
  • What help can I get while I wait?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Statewide

Wisconsin Children's Long-Term Support program: apply through your county (statewide)

For: Children, youth and families, Intellectual or developmental disability

Wisconsin Department of Health Services

The Children's Long-Term Support program serves children with disabilities, including developmental disabilities. You start by contacting your county health and human services agency. A support and service coordinator visits your home, uses an online tool to look at your child's care needs, and helps with enrollment.

Next step: Call your county health and human services agency and say you want services for your child.

Good to know
  • Children with disabilities who meet the program's eligibility rules.

Statewide

Wisconsin programs for adults with developmental and intellectual disabilities (statewide)

For: Intellectual or developmental disability

Wisconsin Department of Health Services

Wisconsin offers several long-term care programs for adults with intellectual or developmental disabilities, including Family Care and IRIS, the self-directed option. You enroll through your local Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC) or tribal aging and disability resource specialist. They complete a functional screen and help with the Medicaid application.

Next step: Contact your local ADRC and ask to start the long-term care functional screen.

Good to know
  • Adults 18 and older who need long-term care and are eligible for Medicaid.
