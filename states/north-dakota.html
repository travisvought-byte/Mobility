Ramps & home modification

Programs that build, lend or pay for ramps, bathroom changes and other home access work.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Whether you own or rent. If you rent, ask the landlord in writing before any work.
  • Photos of the entrance and a count of the steps, or the height from the ground to the door.
  • What the person uses to get around: wheelchair, scooter, walker or cane.
  • Household size and income, if the program is based on income.

Ask

  • Does the work have to be approved before it starts?
  • Is this a grant, a loan or a free build by volunteers?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Statewide

North Dakota rural transit and Dial-A-Ride

North Dakota Department of Transportation

North Dakota’s transit program serves anyone who needs a ride, and about 31 bus programs serve rural North Dakota. Demand-response rides are open to everyone: you call your local provider and reserve a seat. Dial-A-Ride is door-to-door accessible service for people with disabilities and seniors. Some providers also run town-to-town trips to regional hubs and medical rides to treatment such as dialysis or chemotherapy.

Next step: Use the transit providers map or list linked on the page to find the provider for your area, then call and ask about Dial-A-Ride and how far ahead to book.

Good to know
  • Dial-A-Ride is for eligible riders; ask your provider how to qualify.
  • Most Dial-A-Ride trips are booked at least 24 hours ahead; some providers take same-day requests.

Disability parking placard in North Dakota: how to apply

For: Mobility or physical disability

North Dakota Department of Transportation, Motor Vehicle Division

In North Dakota, disability parking placards are issued by the North Dakota Department of Transportation, Motor Vehicle Division. Apply with the Application for Mobility-Impaired Parking Permit (SFN 2886). Who can certify the disability: a physician, chiropractor, physician assistant, advanced practice registered nurse or physical therapist.

Next step: Open the agency’s page for the current form and have the medical part completed. Apply at a motor vehicle office (driver’s license offices do not issue permits) or by mail to the ND Department of Transportation, Motor Vehicle Division, Bismarck, ND 58505-0780. Offices outside Bismarck may add a service fee.

Good to know
  • A temporary (red) permit is issued three months at a time and costs $3.
  • There is no fee for a permanent permit. A permit for a non-reversible condition renews without a new medical signature.
  • Forms, fees and rules change. Check the agency’s page before you apply.

Have ready

  • Your Social Security number and a photo ID.

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (North Dakota)

USDA Rural Development, Bismarck state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the North Dakota Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.

Early intervention for babies and toddlers (birth to age 3)

For: Children, youth and families

Developmental Disabilities Division; Department of Human Services

The early intervention program in North Dakota serves children from birth to age three who have a developmental delay or disability. Parents do not have to wait for a referral, and the evaluation is free.

Next step: Call the referral line. Say you are concerned about your child’s development and want an evaluation under Part C of IDEA. Write down the name of each person you speak with and the date.

Good to know
  • For children under age three. If your child is three or older, ask your school district for a special education evaluation, which is also free.
  • The evaluation and service coordination are free. Depending on state policy, some services may have a sliding-scale fee. A child cannot be denied services because the family cannot pay.
  • For an eligible child, the steps from referral to a written service plan (an Individualized Family Service Plan) should take 45 days.
  • This is the office the ECTA Center, a technical assistance center funded by the U.S. Department of Education, lists for North Dakota under Part C of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA). The points here about referral, cost and timing come from the Center for Parent Information and Resources.

Independent living help for older adults with vision loss in North Dakota

For: Blind or low vision, Older adults

North Dakota Health and Human Services Vocational Rehabilitation (OIB program)

This is North Dakota’s program under the federal Independent Living Services for Older Individuals who are Blind (OIB) grant, run by the North Dakota Health and Human Services Vocational Rehabilitation (OIB program). According to Time To Be Bold, these services often include training in making the most of remaining vision, using magnifiers and other devices, using phones and computers, staying safe and organized at home and getting around the community. Who qualifies, according to the state page: People 55 and older with a visual impairment who are not working or looking for work and who live in their own home or another independent setting, not a nursing home. Services are free, though some devices may cost a little.

Next step: Call, email or send the online referral form, which takes one to two business days to process. Staff then schedule a visit at your home.

Good to know
  • Under the federal program these services are meant for people 55 and older who are not looking for work. If you want to keep or find a job, ask about vocational rehabilitation instead.
  • If there is a waiting list, Time To Be Bold suggests Hadley’s free lessons on living with vision loss, by phone, mail or online: 800-323-4238.

Rights, advocacy & legal help

Disability rights agencies, complaint offices and legal aid for denials, access barriers, school, health care and voting.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • What happened, where, when and who was involved, written down.
  • Copies of letters, emails, notices and photos.
  • What you asked for and the answer you got.
  • The date on any denial or notice. Deadlines can be short.

Ask

  • Does a law cover this, and which office enforces it?
  • What is my deadline to complain or appeal?
  • Can you represent me, or only advise me?
  • What should I put in writing right now?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Statewide

North Dakota State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

North Dakota Aging Services Division

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.
