Ramps & home modification

Programs that build, lend or pay for ramps, bathroom changes and other home access work.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Whether you own or rent. If you rent, ask the landlord in writing before any work.
  • Photos of the entrance and a count of the steps, or the height from the ground to the door.
  • What the person uses to get around: wheelchair, scooter, walker or cane.
  • Household size and income, if the program is based on income.

Ask

  • Does the work have to be approved before it starts?
  • Is this a grant, a loan or a free build by volunteers?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • The make, model and serial number of the equipment, if you have them.
  • Who paid for it and when: Medicare, Medicaid, the VA, private insurance or you.
  • The name of the supplier or dealer, and any warranty papers.

Ask

  • Can I borrow or try equipment while I wait?
  • Is there a cost, and can it be reduced or waived?
  • How long is the wait, and who do I call to check on it?
  • Do you serve my county?

Statewide

Disability parking placard in Ohio: how to apply

For: Mobility or physical disability

Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV)

In Ohio, disability parking placards are issued by the Ohio Bureau of Motor Vehicles (BMV). Apply with the Application for Removable Windshield Placards (BMV 4826). Who can certify the disability: a health care provider, who writes a prescription giving the date, your name, a statement that it is for a removable windshield placard, how long the condition is expected to last, and the provider’s signature.

Next step: Open the agency’s page for the current form and have the medical part completed. Bring the application and prescription to a deputy registrar license agency.

Good to know
  • Veterans and active-duty service members with a service-connected disability that limits walking can get a free placard with form BMV 4531.
  • Forms, fees and rules change. Check the agency’s page before you apply.

How to reach them: Apply through the agency’s page, which lists its offices and how to reach them.

Ohio Department of Transportation

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • A list of what you need help with each day, such as bathing, meals or getting out of bed.

Ask

  • How many hours of help can I get, and who decides?
  • Is there a waiting list?

Early intervention for babies and toddlers (birth to age 3)

For: Children, youth and families

Ohio Department of Children and Youth

The early intervention program in Ohio serves children from birth to age three who have a developmental delay or disability. Parents do not have to wait for a referral, and the evaluation is free.

Next step: Use the state’s referral page, linked here, or call the program office and ask how to refer your child where you live. Say you are concerned about your child’s development and want an evaluation under Part C of IDEA. Write down the name of each person you speak with and the date.

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including Ohio. The Ohio page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.
