Assistive Technology of Alaska (ATLA) for Alaska DVR

Telecommunications Equipment Distribution Program (TEDP): phone equipment for hearing loss

Telecommunications Equipment Distribution Program (TEDP)

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as hearing loss certified by a hearing health professional. Equipment is loaned.

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Alaska Department of Transportation and Public Facilities: rural public transit program (Section 5311)

Alaska Department of Transportation and Public Facilities

The Alaska Department of Transportation and Public Facilities runs the federal Section 5311 program, which funds public transit in towns and rural areas with fewer than 50,000 people. Rural transit systems in this state are paid for in part through this office, and many offer rides that people with disabilities can use.

Next step: Ask the state transit office, or look on the page, for the public transit provider that serves your county, then call that provider and ask about door-to-door rides and how to register.

Good to know
  • This is a state grants office, not a ride booking line.
  • Rural transit hours, fares and service areas are set by each local provider.

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (AK)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says Alaska runs rides directly, with a statewide call center run by an administrative contractor (information collected Sep 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.

Statewide

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (AK)

Alaska Department of Transportation and Public Facilities

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. The state passes the money to eligible nonprofits, public agencies and transit operators, which run the vehicles and rides.

Next step: If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging, Center for Independent Living or local transit agency which 5310-funded rides serve your county. If you run a nonprofit or transit service, use this page to see how and when to apply.

Good to know
  • Funds go to eligible organizations, so riders use the services those groups run rather than applying for money themselves.
  • Application rounds, deadlines and forms change; check the page for the current cycle.

Statewide

Alaska Division of Vocational Rehabilitation

The state vocational rehabilitation agency helps people with disabilities prepare for, find and keep work. When equipment is needed for a work goal it can pay for assistive technology and vehicle modifications, and in some states home modifications. Eligibility and the order of service vary by state.

Next step: Call to apply, and ask about assistive technology, vehicle modification and home modification funding before you buy anything.

Good to know
  • Services must connect to an employment goal.
  • Apply and get approval before buying equipment or starting modifications.
  • Some states keep a waitlist or order of selection.

Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Alaska)

USDA Rural Development, Palmer state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Alaska Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.
Call (907) 761-7705Official page

Statewide

Environmental Modification (E-Mod) Home Accessibility Program

Alaska Division of Senior and Disabilities Services

Helps Alaska Medicaid waiver participants make home modifications. HomeMods.org lists a cost cap of $40,000 every 3 years, with project estimates sent by care coordinators for state approval. Four waivers include environmental modifications: Alaskans Living Independently; Adults with Physical and Developmental Disabilities; Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities; Children with Complex Medical Conditions.

Next step: Ask your care coordinator to send a project estimate for approval before any work starts.

Good to know
  • Open to people on an Alaska Medicaid waiver.
  • Cost cap of $40,000 every 3 years.

Aging & disability navigation

Statewide

Alaska State Library Talking Book Center: talking-book and braille library service

Alaska State Library Talking Book Center

Alaska State Library Talking Book Center in Juneau is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving Alaska. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 888-820-4525, say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

Good to know
  • NLS says this center processes applications; the Utah library provides materials and help with BARD downloads.
  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.
Call 888-820-4525Official page

Main line as listed in the Library of Congress NLS Find a Library directory; library website as listed: talkingbooks.alaska.gov

Statewide

iCanConnect in Alaska: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), Alaska program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including Alaska. The Alaska page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the Alaska page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.
Call 800-825-4595Official page

National iCanConnect line; the Alaska page lists the local program contact

University of Alaska Anchorage

Center for Human Development is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at University of Alaska Anchorage. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

Alaska Department of Health

Alaska Traumatic and Acquired Brain Injury Program is run by or through Alaska Department of Health, which the National Association of State Head Injury Administrators lists as the state lead on brain injury. State brain injury programs coordinate services, trust fund or waiver supports, public information and the state advisory council for people with traumatic or acquired brain injury, including veterans and people hurt in crashes, falls or sports.

Rights, advocacy & legal help

Statewide

Alaska State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Alaska Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.

Northwest ADA Center

University of Washington

The Northwest ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by University of Washington.

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.

Developmental disability agencies

Statewide

Alaska Developmental Disabilities Resource Connection (DDRC) and ADRC: where to start for waivers (statewide)

Alaska Department of Health, Division of Senior and Disabilities Services

The DDRC is the first stop for Alaskans with an intellectual or developmental disability and their families. DDRC staff can help you get on the waiting list (the DD Registry) for the IDD and Individualized Supports waivers. An Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC) can refer you to a DDRC. After you are drawn from the list, a care coordinator helps you apply.

Next step: Call the ADRC and DDRC toll-free line and ask to start intake.

Good to know
  • You must qualify for Medicaid and meet an intermediate care facility level of care for the IDD and Individualized Supports waivers.
Call 1-855-565-2017Official page

ADRC and DDRC toll-free; Division of Senior and Disabilities Services in Anchorage 907-269-3666 or 800-478-9996
