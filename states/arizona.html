Equipment, repair & assistive technology

Statewide

Arizona Technology Access Program (AzTAP)

This is the state Assistive Technology Act program, funded by the federal Administration for Community Living. It lets you try equipment before you buy (device demonstration), borrow it short term (device loan), get refurbished used equipment at low or no cost (reuse), and in many states apply for a low-interest financial loan. It serves people of all ages with any disability.

Next step: Call and ask about borrowing or trying a device, the reuse or exchange program, and any financial loan for equipment you need.

Good to know
  • Equipment and services offered vary by state and by program funding.

Arizona Telecommunications Equipment Distribution Program (AzTEDP): phone equipment for hearing loss

Arizona Telecommunications Equipment Distribution Program (AzTEDP)

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as hearing loss certified by a designated professional; the list says applicants must be U.S. citizens. Equipment is loaned.

Next step: Open the website, check the current eligibility and application steps, and ask a hearing health professional to certify your hearing loss if the program requires it.

Good to know
  • Eligibility, income limits and equipment rules change; confirm on the program page before you apply.
  • Some states also cover speech or mobility disabilities; the program page says which.

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Arizona Department of Transportation: rural public transit program (Section 5311)

Arizona Department of Transportation

The Arizona Department of Transportation runs the federal Section 5311 program, which funds public transit in towns and rural areas with fewer than 50,000 people. Rural transit systems in this state are paid for in part through this office, and many offer rides that people with disabilities can use.

Next step: Ask the state transit office, or look on the page, for the public transit provider that serves your county, then call that provider and ask about door-to-door rides and how to register.

Good to know
  • This is a state grants office, not a ride booking line.
  • Rural transit hours, fares and service areas are set by each local provider.

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (AZ)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says Arizona runs rides directly and through regional brokers that work with managed care plans (information collected Sep 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.

Statewide

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (AZ)

Arizona Department of Transportation

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. The state passes the money to eligible nonprofits, public agencies and transit operators, which run the vehicles and rides.

Next step: If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging, Center for Independent Living or local transit agency which 5310-funded rides serve your county. If you run a nonprofit or transit service, use this page to see how and when to apply.

Good to know
  • Funds go to eligible organizations, so riders use the services those groups run rather than applying for money themselves.
  • Application rounds, deadlines and forms change; check the page for the current cycle.

Coverage & funding

Statewide

Arizona Rehabilitation Services

The state vocational rehabilitation agency helps people with disabilities prepare for, find and keep work. When equipment is needed for a work goal it can pay for assistive technology and vehicle modifications, and in some states home modifications. Eligibility and the order of service vary by state.

Next step: Call to apply, and ask about assistive technology, vehicle modification and home modification funding before you buy anything.

Good to know
  • Services must connect to an employment goal.
  • Apply and get approval before buying equipment or starting modifications.
  • Some states keep a waitlist or order of selection.

Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Arizona)

USDA Rural Development, Phoenix state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Arizona Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.

Statewide

Arizona Long Term Care System (ALTCS): home modifications that remove barriers

Arizona Health Care Cost Containment System (AHCCCS)

Medicaid long-term care program for people 65 or older, or with a disability, who need nursing facility level of care. Covers home modifications that remove barriers, only as changes to an existing structure and not additions.

Next step: Ask AHCCCS or your ALTCS case manager how to apply and whether a modification can go in your plan before work starts.

Good to know
  • Changes to an existing structure only, not additions.
  • Owned or family-owned homes where the person lives; for rentals the owner must consent in writing and may require the original condition to be restored.

Aging & disability navigation

Statewide

Arizona State Braille and Talking Book Library: talking-book and braille library service

Arizona State Braille and Talking Book Library

Arizona State Braille and Talking Book Library in Phoenix is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving Arizona. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 602-255-5578 (in-state toll-free 800-255-5578), say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

Good to know
  • Braille readers in Arizona are served by the Utah library, per NLS.
  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.
Call 602-255-5578Official page

Main line as listed in the Library of Congress NLS Find a Library directory; in-state toll-free 800-255-5578; library website as listed: www.azlibrary.gov/talkingbooks

Statewide

iCanConnect in Arizona: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), Arizona program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including Arizona. The Arizona page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the Arizona page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.
Call 800-825-4595Official page

National iCanConnect line; the Arizona page lists the local program contact

Arizona UCEDD (Institute for Human Development)

Northern Arizona University

Arizona UCEDD (Institute for Human Development) is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at Northern Arizona University. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

Arizona Governor's Council on Spinal and Head Injuries

Arizona Department of Economic Security

Arizona Governor's Council on Spinal and Head Injuries is run by or through Arizona Department of Economic Security, which the National Association of State Head Injury Administrators lists as the state lead on brain injury. State brain injury programs coordinate services, trust fund or waiver supports, public information and the state advisory council for people with traumatic or acquired brain injury, including veterans and people hurt in crashes, falls or sports. Rights, advocacy & legal help

Statewide

Arizona State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Arizona Division of Aging and Adult Services

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.

Statewide

Pacific ADA Center

The Pacific ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by Pacific ADA Center.

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.
