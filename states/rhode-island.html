Ramps & home modification

Programs that build, lend or pay for ramps, bathroom changes and other home access work.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Whether you own or rent. If you rent, ask the landlord in writing before any work.
  • Photos of the entrance and a count of the steps, or the height from the ground to the door.
  • What the person uses to get around: wheelchair, scooter, walker or cane.
  • Household size and income, if the program is based on income.

Ask

  • Does the work have to be approved before it starts?
  • Is this a grant, a loan or a free build by volunteers?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • The make, model and serial number of the equipment, if you have them.
  • Who paid for it and when: Medicare, Medicaid, the VA, private insurance or you.
  • The name of the supplier or dealer, and any warranty papers.
  • What is wrong, when it started and what it keeps you from doing.

Ask

  • Can I borrow or try equipment while I wait?
  • Is there a cost, and can it be reduced or waived?
  • How long is the wait, and who do I call to check on it?
  • Do you serve my county?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Disability parking placard in Rhode Island: how to apply

For: Mobility or physical disability

Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles, Disability Parking Placards Office

In Rhode Island, disability parking placards are issued by the Rhode Island Division of Motor Vehicles, Disability Parking Placards Office. Apply with the New/Renew Disability Parking Placard Application. Who can certify the disability: your physician.

Next step: Open the agency’s page for the current form and have the medical part completed. Mail it to RI DMV, Disability Parking Placards Office, 600 New London Avenue, Cranston, RI 02920-3024, fax it to 401-462-0829, or bring it to the Cranston headquarters, within 30 days of the physician’s certification.

Good to know
  • A temporary (red) placard lasts 2 to 12 months.
  • Long-term placards last one to three years and permanent placards six years.
  • Veterans with a 100% service-connected disability use a separate veteran placard application.
  • Forms, fees and rules change. Check the agency’s page before you apply.

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Rhode Island)

USDA Rural Development, Amherst, MA state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Rhode Island Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

Ask

  • How many hours of help can I get, and who decides?
  • Is there a waiting list?
  • What do I do if my helper does not show up?
  • For energy bill help: when are applications taken, and is there faster help if I have a shut-off notice?

Statewide

Early intervention for babies and toddlers (birth to age 3)

For: Children, youth and families

Executive Office of Health and Human Services; Center for Child and Family Health

The early intervention program in Rhode Island serves children from birth to age three who have a developmental delay or disability. Parents do not have to wait for a referral, and the evaluation is free.

Next step: Call the program office, or use its website, and ask how to refer your child where you live. Say you are concerned about your child’s development and want an evaluation under Part C of IDEA. Write down the name of each person you speak with and the date.

Good to know
  • For children under age three. If your child is three or older, ask your school district for a special education evaluation, which is also free.
  • The evaluation and service coordination are free. Depending on state policy, some services may have a sliding-scale fee. A child cannot be denied services because the family cannot pay.
  • For an eligible child, the steps from referral to a written service plan (an Individualized Family Service Plan) should take 45 days.
  • This is the office the ECTA Center, a technical assistance center funded by the U.S. Department of Education, lists for Rhode Island under Part C of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA). The points here about referral, cost and timing come from the Center for Parent Information and Resources.

Vision, hearing & communication

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • A recent eye exam or hearing test, if you have one.
  • What has become hard to do: reading mail, using the phone, getting around, working.
  • Proof of where you live and of income, if the program asks.
  • How you want to be contacted: voice, text, email, videophone or relay.

Ask

  • Do you come to my home, or do I travel to you?
  • Is the equipment free, loaned or sold?
  • Who can sign the form that confirms my vision or hearing loss?
  • Is there training for my family too?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Independent living help for older adults with vision loss in Rhode Island

For: Blind or low vision, Older adults

Rhode Island Services for the Blind and Visually Impaired (Older Blind Independent Living Program)

This is Rhode Island’s program under the federal Independent Living Services for Older Individuals who are Blind (OIB) grant, run by the Rhode Island Services for the Blind and Visually Impaired (Older Blind Independent Living Program). Paul V. Sherlock Center on Disabilities

For: Intellectual or developmental disability

Rhode Island College

Paul V. Sherlock Center on Disabilities is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at Rhode Island College. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

Next step: Call the center and ask what community projects, assistive technology help, or referrals it offers for adults and children with developmental disabilities in your area.

Good to know
  • Services vary by center and funding; many UCEDDs focus on training, research and referral rather than ongoing direct care.

For: Brain injury or stroke

Rhode Island Department of Health

Rhode Island Traumatic Brain Injury Program is run by or through Rhode Island Department of Health, which the National Association of State Head Injury Administrators lists as the state lead on brain injury. Rights, advocacy & legal help

Disability rights agencies, complaint offices and legal aid for denials, access barriers, school, health care and voting.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • What happened, where, when and who was involved, written down.
  • Copies of letters, emails, notices and photos.
  • What you asked for and the answer you got.
  • The date on any denial or notice. Deadlines can be short.

Ask

  • Does a law cover this, and which office enforces it?
  • What is my deadline to complain or appeal?
  • Can you represent me, or only advise me?
  • What should I put in writing right now?

Statewide

New England ADA Center

Institute for Human Centered Design

The New England ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by Institute for Human Centered Design.

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.

Statewide

Rhode Island State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Alliance for Better Long-Term Care

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.

Statewide

Rhode Island Adult Protective Services: report abuse, neglect or exploitation

Rhode Island Adult Protective Services

The reporting line in Rhode Island for suspected abuse, neglect or financial exploitation of adults ages 18 to 59 with developmental disabilities, mental health or substance use conditions. Adult Protective Services takes the report, decides whether it meets the state’s rules and looks into it. You can make a report without giving your name.

Next step: Call and say who is at risk, where they are and what you have seen or been told. Ask what happens next and whether you can get an update.

Good to know
  • For adults 60 and older, call the Office of Healthy Aging: 401-462-3000.
  • In immediate danger, call 911.

County boards, regional centers and state offices for developmental disabilities. They decide eligibility, coordinate services and manage waiver funding.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

Ask

Rhode Island Division of Developmental Disabilities: eligibility and application (statewide)

For: Intellectual or developmental disability

Rhode Island Department of Behavioral Healthcare, Developmental Disabilities and Hospitals

You apply to the Division of Developmental Disabilities for adult services. Students can apply 2 months before their 17th birthday, and a decision comes within 30 days of a complete application. You also need Medicaid eligibility to start services. The Rhode Island Parent Information Network can help you fill out the application at 401-270-0101.
