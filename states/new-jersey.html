New Jersey Equipment Distribution Program: phone equipment for hearing loss

New Jersey Equipment Distribution Program

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as hearing loss verified by a physician or audiologist and income within the family-size limit. Equipment is owned by the recipient.

Next step: Open the website, check the current eligibility and application steps, and ask a hearing health professional to certify your hearing loss if the program requires it.

Good to know
  • Eligibility, income limits and equipment rules change; confirm on the program page before you apply.
  • Some states also cover speech or mobility disabilities; the program page says which.

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (NJ)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says New Jersey uses a statewide broker, Modivcare (information collected Sep 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.

Statewide

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (NJ)

NJ TRANSIT

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. The state passes the money to eligible nonprofits, public agencies and transit operators, which run the vehicles and rides.

Next step: If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging, Center for Independent Living or local transit agency which 5310-funded rides serve your county. If you run a nonprofit or transit service, use this page to see how and when to apply.

Good to know
  • Funds go to eligible organizations, so riders use the services those groups run rather than applying for money themselves.
  • Application rounds, deadlines and forms change; check the page for the current cycle.

New Jersey Division of Vocational Rehabilitation Services

The state vocational rehabilitation agency helps people with disabilities prepare for, find and keep work. When equipment is needed for a work goal it can pay for assistive technology and vehicle modifications, and in some states home modifications. Eligibility and the order of service vary by state.

Next step: Call to apply, and ask about assistive technology, vehicle modification and home modification funding before you buy anything.

Good to know

Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (New Jersey)

USDA Rural Development, Mount Laurel state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the New Jersey Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.
Call (856) 787-7700Official page

Statewide

iCanConnect in New Jersey: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), New Jersey program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including New Jersey. The New Jersey page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the New Jersey page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.
Call 800-825-4595Official page

National iCanConnect line; the New Jersey page lists the local program contact

New Jersey Commission for the Blind and Visually Impaired: state agency for blind and low-vision residents

New Jersey Commission for the Blind and Visually Impaired

New Jersey Commission for the Blind and Visually Impaired is New Jersey's state agency for people who are blind or visually impaired. The U.S. Department of Education's Rehabilitation Services Administration lists it as a blind-type vocational rehabilitation agency, so it is a state route to rehabilitation services for blind and low-vision residents of New Jersey.

Next step: Call the number listed, say you are blind or have low vision, and ask how to apply, what services the agency offers, and who your first contact will be. If you use a relay service, use the TTY number or the agency website.

Good to know
  • Listing comes from a federal directory; confirm eligibility, services and intake steps with the agency.
  • Some states split services between a blind agency and a general vocational rehabilitation agency; ask which one handles your situation.

New Jersey State Library Talking Book and Braille Center

New Jersey State Library Talking Book and Braille Center in Trenton is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. Rutgers Robert Wood Johnson Medical School

Boggs Center on Disability and Human Development is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at Rutgers Robert Wood Johnson Medical School. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

  • Services vary by center and funding; many UCEDDs focus on training, research and referral rather than ongoing direct care.

New Jersey Department of Human Services, Division of Disability Services

New Jersey Traumatic Brain Injury Program is run by or through New Jersey Department of Human Services, Division of Disability Services, which the National Association of State Head Injury Administrators lists as the state lead on brain injury. Rights, advocacy & legal help

Statewide

New Jersey State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

New Jersey Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.

Statewide

Northeast ADA Center

K. Lisa Yang and Hock E. Tan Institute on Employment and Disability, Cornell University ILR School

The Northeast ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by K. Lisa Yang and Hock E. Tan Institute on Employment and Disability, Cornell University ILR School.

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.
