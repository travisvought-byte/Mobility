Ramps & home modification

Programs that build, lend or pay for ramps, bathroom changes and other home access work.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Whether you own or rent. If you rent, ask the landlord in writing before any work.
  • Photos of the entrance and a count of the steps, or the height from the ground to the door.
  • What the person uses to get around: wheelchair, scooter, walker or cane.
  • Household size and income, if the program is based on income.

Ask

  • Does the work have to be approved before it starts?
  • Is this a grant, a loan or a free build by volunteers?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Disability parking placard in New York: how to apply

For: Mobility or physical disability

Local city, town or village issuing agents (New York State DMV issues plates only)

In New York, disability parking permits are issued by local city, town or village offices, usually the clerk. The state DMV issues disability plates only. Apply with the Application for a Parking Permit or License Plates for Persons with Severe Disabilities (MV-664.1). Who can certify the disability: a physician, physician assistant, nurse practitioner, podiatrist (for foot-related disabilities) or optometrist (for blindness), on the form or on letterhead.

Next step: Open the agency’s page for the current form and have the medical part completed. Take it to the office that issues permits where you live, usually your city, town or village clerk. Do not send it to DMV, which does not issue parking permits. New York City residents mail it to the NYC Department of Transportation.

Good to know
  • There is no fee for a permit.
  • New York City residents can also get a separate city permit for curbside parking from NYC DOT (718-433-3100). Outside New York City, the same local office issues metered parking waivers (form MV-664.1MP).
  • Forms, fees and rules change. Check the agency’s page before you apply.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Your Social Security number and a photo ID.
  • Names, addresses and dates for your doctors, clinics and hospitals, and a list of your medicines.

Ask

  • Which program fits my situation, and can I apply for more than one?
  • What is the deadline to appeal, and where do I send the appeal?
  • Will working or saving money change my benefits?
  • Who can help me fill out the forms at no cost?

USDA Rural Development, Syracuse state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards.   • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.
