Virginia Assistive Technology Act program

Virginia Technical Assistance Program (TAP): phone equipment for hearing loss

Virginia Technical Assistance Program (TAP)

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as income within the family-size limit. Equipment is 30-day trial loan; kept equipment is owned, except items over $5,000 stay on permanent loan.

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (VA)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says Virginia uses a statewide broker, Modivcare, plus brokers hired by managed care plans (Modivcare, Access2Care, MTM and Verida) (information collected Sep 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.
Official page

How to reach them: Call the member services number printed on your Medicaid card.

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (VA)

Virginia Department of Rail and Public Transportation

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Virginia)

USDA Rural Development, Richmond state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Virginia Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.

iCanConnect in Virginia: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), Virginia program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including Virginia. The Virginia page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the Virginia page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.

Virginia Department for the Blind and Vision Impaired Library: talking-book and braille library service

Virginia Department for the Blind and Vision Impaired Library

Virginia Department for the Blind and Vision Impaired Library in Richmond is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving Virginia. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 804-371-3661 (in-state toll-free 800-552-7015) or use the library website, say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

  • Several Virginia localities are also served by local talking-book libraries listed separately.
  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.

Mid-Atlantic ADA Center

TransCen, Inc.

The Mid-Atlantic ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. Statewide

Virginia State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Virginia Department for Aging and Rehabilitative Services

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.
