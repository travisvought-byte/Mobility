Equipment, repair & assistive technology

Statewide

DC SHARES and DC Assistive Technology Program

DC Assistive Technology Program (University Legal Services)

DC SHARES collects and distributes used manual wheelchairs, walkers, lifts, crutches, canes, commodes and shower chairs free to DC residents. The DC Assistive Technology Program also runs a device loan library, a cane and walker clinic and a financing loan program.

Next step: Call the DC Assistive Technology Program at (202) 547-0198.

Good to know
  • Powered equipment is generally not collected; ask about availability.

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (DC)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says the District of Columbia uses a statewide broker, MTM, plus brokers hired by managed care organizations (information collected Sep 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.
Official page

How to reach them: Call the member services number printed on your Medicaid card.

District Department of Transportation (DDOT)

DDOT administers the federal Section 5310 program for the District. Its management plan describes providing federally funded vehicles to eligible organizations that serve older adults and people with disabilities.

Next step: If you run an eligible nonprofit, ask DDOT for the current Section 5310 forms and application dates. If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging or the DC Office on Aging.

Coverage & funding

Statewide

DC Vocational Rehabilitation Services

The state vocational rehabilitation agency helps people with disabilities prepare for, find and keep work. When equipment is needed for a work goal it can pay for assistive technology and vehicle modifications, and in some states home modifications. Eligibility and the order of service vary by state.

Next step: Call to apply, and ask about assistive technology, vehicle modification and home modification funding before you buy anything.

Good to know
  • Services must connect to an employment goal.
  • Apply and get approval before buying equipment or starting modifications.
  • Some states keep a waitlist or order of selection.

Aging & disability navigation

Statewide

Center for Accessibility (DC Public Library): talking-book and braille library service

Center for Accessibility (DC Public Library)

Center for Accessibility (DC Public Library) in Washington is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving the District of Columbia. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 202-727-2142, say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

Good to know
  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.
Call 202-727-2142Official page

Main line as listed in the Library of Congress NLS Find a Library directory; library website as listed: dclibrary.org/services/accessibility

Statewide

iCanConnect in District of Columbia: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), District of Columbia program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including District of Columbia. The District of Columbia page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the District of Columbia page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.
Call 800-825-4595Official page

National iCanConnect line; the District of Columbia page lists the local program contact

Every state has a governor-appointed Council on Developmental Disabilities, made up mostly of people with developmental disabilities and their family members. DC Department of Health, Bureau of Epidemiology and Health Risk

DC Traumatic Brain Injury Program is run by or through DC Department of Health, Bureau of Epidemiology and Health Risk, which the National Association of State Head Injury Administrators lists as the state lead on brain injury. Georgetown UCEDD/LEND

Georgetown University

Georgetown UCEDD/LEND is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at Georgetown University. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

Next step: Call the center and ask what community projects, assistive technology help, or referrals it offers for adults and children with developmental disabilities in your area.

Rights, advocacy & legal help

Statewide

District of Columbia State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Legal Counsel for the Elderly

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.
Call 202-434-3038Official page

From the National Ombudsman Resource Center state list dated July 2023; confirm before calling

Statewide

Mid-Atlantic ADA Center

TransCen, Inc.

The Mid-Atlantic ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by TransCen, Inc..

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.
Call 301-217-0124Official page
