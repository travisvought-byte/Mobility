Ramps & home modification

Programs that build, lend or pay for ramps, bathroom changes and other home access work.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Whether you own or rent. If you rent, ask the landlord in writing before any work.
  • Photos of the entrance and a count of the steps, or the height from the ground to the door.
  • What the person uses to get around: wheelchair, scooter, walker or cane.
  • Household size and income, if the program is based on income.

Ask

  • Does the work have to be approved before it starts?
  • Is this a grant, a loan or a free build by volunteers?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Equipment, repair & assistive technology

Try, borrow, repair, finance or reuse wheelchairs and assistive technology.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • The make, model and serial number of the equipment, if you have them.
  • Who paid for it and when: Medicare, Medicaid, the VA, private insurance or you.
  • The name of the supplier or dealer, and any warranty papers.
  • What is wrong, when it started and what it keeps you from doing.

Ask

  • Can I borrow or try equipment while I wait?
  • Is there a cost, and can it be reduced or waived?
  • How long is the wait, and who do I call to check on it?
  • Do you serve my county?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Disability parking placard in Nevada: how to apply

For: Mobility or physical disability

Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV)

In Nevada, disability parking placards are issued by the Nevada Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV). Apply with the Disabled Persons License Plates and/or Placards Application (SP-27). Who can certify the disability: a licensed physician, advanced practice registered nurse, physician assistant, or physical or occupational therapist.

Next step: Open the agency’s page for the current form and have the medical part completed. Request a placard online in DriveNV ("Request ADA Products"), fax a placard-only application to 775-684-4797, or apply at a DMV office. First-time plate applications must be made in person.

Good to know
  • Temporary and moderate-duration placards are certified for a set time of no more than two years; renewing needs a new application and signature.
  • There is no fee for placards.
  • Nevada also issues an Expedited Service Permit, valid ten years, that gives people with an irreversible disability faster service at state agencies; apply in person at a full-service DMV office.
  • Forms, fees and rules change. Check the agency’s page before you apply.

Income, benefits & health coverage

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Your Social Security number and a photo ID.
  • Names, addresses and dates for your doctors, clinics and hospitals, and a list of your medicines.
  • Recent pay stubs, benefit letters and bank statements.
  • Any denial or decision letter. Note the date on it, because appeal deadlines are counted from the day you receive it.

Ask

  • Which program fits my situation, and can I apply for more than one?
  • What is the deadline to appeal, and where do I send the appeal?
  • Will working or saving money change my benefits?
  • Who can help me fill out the forms at no cost?

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Nevada)

USDA Rural Development, Carson City state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Nevada Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.

Early intervention for babies and toddlers (birth to age 3)

For: Children, youth and families

Nevada Department of Health and Human Services; Aging and Disability Services Division

The early intervention program in Nevada serves children from birth to age three who have a developmental delay or disability. Parents do not have to wait for a referral, and the evaluation is free.

Next step: Call the referral line. Say you are concerned about your child’s development and want an evaluation under Part C of IDEA. Write down the name of each person you speak with and the date.

Good to know
  • For children under age three. If your child is three or older, ask your school district for a special education evaluation, which is also free.
  • The evaluation and service coordination are free. Depending on state policy, some services may have a sliding-scale fee. A child cannot be denied services because the family cannot pay.
  • For an eligible child, the steps from referral to a written service plan (an Individualized Family Service Plan) should take 45 days.
  • This is the office the ECTA Center, a technical assistance center funded by the U.S. Department of Education, lists for Nevada under Part C of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA). The points here about referral, cost and timing come from the Center for Parent Information and Resources.

Independent living help for older adults with vision loss in Nevada

For: Blind or low vision, Older adults

Nevada Bureau of Services to the Blind and Visually Impaired (BSBVI)

This is Nevada’s program under the federal Independent Living Services for Older Individuals who are Blind (OIB) grant, run by the Nevada Bureau of Services to the Blind and Visually Impaired (BSBVI). According to Time To Be Bold, these services often include training in making the most of remaining vision, using magnifiers and other devices, using phones and computers, staying safe and organized at home and getting around the community. University of Nevada, Reno

Nevada Center for Excellence in Disabilities is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at University of Nevada, Reno. Rights, advocacy & legal help

Disability rights agencies, complaint offices and legal aid for denials, access barriers, school, health care and voting.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • What happened, where, when and who was involved, written down.
  • Copies of letters, emails, notices and photos.
  • What you asked for and the answer you got.
  • The date on any denial or notice. Deadlines can be short.

Ask

  • Does a law cover this, and which office enforces it?
  • What is my deadline to complain or appeal?
  • Can you represent me, or only advise me?
  • What should I put in writing right now?

Statewide

Nevada State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Nevada Aging and Disability Services Division

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.

Pacific ADA Center

The Pacific ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by Pacific ADA Center.
