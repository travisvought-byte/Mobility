Assistive Technology in New Hampshire, UNH Institute on Disability

ATinNH runs a refurbish and reuse network with partners that offer long-term loans of donated equipment, such as the Chapin Senior Center in New London. AT4All NH lists equipment for loan, demonstration and reuse.

Statewide

Assistive Technology in New Hampshire

This is the state Assistive Technology Act program, funded by the federal Administration for Community Living. It lets you try equipment before you buy (device demonstration), borrow it short term (device loan), get refurbished used equipment at low or no cost (reuse), and in many states apply for a low-interest financial loan. It serves people of all ages with any disability.

Next step: Call and ask about borrowing or trying a device, the reuse or exchange program, and any financial loan for equipment you need.

Good to know
  • Equipment and services offered vary by state and by program funding.

Statewide

New Hampshire Telecommunications Equipment Assistance Program (TEAP): phone equipment for hearing loss

New Hampshire Telecommunications Equipment Assistance Program (TEAP)

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as the list says to contact the program by email. Equipment is full aid if you are income-eligible; otherwise you repay 50% of the equipment cost.

Next step: Open the website, check the current eligibility and application steps, and ask a hearing health professional to certify your hearing loss if the program requires it.

Good to know
  • Eligibility, income limits and equipment rules change; confirm on the program page before you apply.
  • Some states also cover speech or mobility disabilities; the program page says which.

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (NH)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says New Hampshire uses a statewide broker, CTS, plus brokers hired by managed care organizations (MTM and CTS) (information collected Sep 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.

Statewide

New Hampshire Department of Transportation: rural public transit program (Section 5311)

New Hampshire Department of Transportation

The New Hampshire Department of Transportation runs the federal Section 5311 program, which funds public transit in towns and rural areas with fewer than 50,000 people. Rural transit systems in this state are paid for in part through this office, and many offer rides that people with disabilities can use.

Next step: Ask the state transit office, or look on the page, for the public transit provider that serves your county, then call that provider and ask about door-to-door rides and how to register.

Good to know
  • This is a state grants office, not a ride booking line.
  • Rural transit hours, fares and service areas are set by each local provider.

Statewide

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (NH)

New Hampshire Department of Transportation

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. The state passes the money to eligible nonprofits, public agencies and transit operators, which run the vehicles and rides.

Next step: If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging, Center for Independent Living or local transit agency which 5310-funded rides serve your county. If you run a nonprofit or transit service, use this page to see how and when to apply.

Good to know
  • Funds go to eligible organizations, so riders use the services those groups run rather than applying for money themselves.
  • Application rounds, deadlines and forms change; check the page for the current cycle.

Coverage & funding

Statewide

New Hampshire Bureau of Vocational Rehabilitation

The state vocational rehabilitation agency helps people with disabilities prepare for, find and keep work. When equipment is needed for a work goal it can pay for assistive technology and vehicle modifications, and in some states home modifications. Eligibility and the order of service vary by state.

Next step: Call to apply, and ask about assistive technology, vehicle modification and home modification funding before you buy anything.

Good to know
  • Services must connect to an employment goal.
  • Apply and get approval before buying equipment or starting modifications.
  • Some states keep a waitlist or order of selection.

Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (New Hampshire)

USDA Rural Development, Concord, NH state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the New Hampshire Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.
Call (603) 223-5769Official page

State office main line from the USDA Rural Development state office directory; confirm before calling. This office serves more than one state

Aging & disability navigation

Statewide

iCanConnect in New Hampshire: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), New Hampshire program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including New Hampshire. The New Hampshire page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the New Hampshire page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.
Call 800-825-4595Official page

National iCanConnect line; the New Hampshire page lists the local program contact

Statewide

New Hampshire State Library, Talking Book Services: talking-book and braille library service

New Hampshire State Library, Talking Book Services

New Hampshire State Library, Talking Book Services in Concord is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving New Hampshire. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 603-271-3429 (in-state toll-free 800-491-4200) or use the library website, say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

Good to know
  • Braille readers in New Hampshire are served by the Massachusetts library (Perkins), per NLS.
  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.
Call 603-271-3429Official page

Institute on Disability

University of New Hampshire

Institute on Disability is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at University of New Hampshire. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

Next step: Call the center and ask what community projects, assistive technology help, or referrals it offers for adults and children with developmental disabilities in your area.

Good to know
  • Services vary by center and funding; many UCEDDs focus on training, research and referral rather than ongoing direct care.

Statewide

New Hampshire Acquired Brain Disorder Services

New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services, Bureau of Developmental Services

New Hampshire Acquired Brain Disorder Services is run by or through New Hampshire Department of Health and Human Services, Bureau of Developmental Services, which the National Association of State Head Injury Administrators lists as the state lead on brain injury. State brain injury programs coordinate services, trust fund or waiver supports, public information and the state advisory council for people with traumatic or acquired brain injury, including veterans and people hurt in crashes, falls or sports. The statewide advocacy group is the Brain Injury Association of New Hampshire.

Next step: Contact the program and ask about brain injury resource facilitation, any trust fund or waiver services you may qualify for, and local support groups.

Good to know
  • Services and eligibility vary a lot by state; many are funded by trust funds or Medicaid waivers with limited slots.

Rights, advocacy & legal help

Statewide

New England ADA Center

Institute for Human Centered Design

The New England ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by Institute for Human Centered Design.

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.
Call 617-695-1225Official page

Voice and relay number from the ADA National Network region list.

Statewide

New Hampshire State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

New Hampshire Office of the Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.
