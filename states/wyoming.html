Wyoming Assistive Technology Resources: loans and reuse

Wyoming Institute for Disabilities, University of Wyoming

WATR lends a wide variety of assistive devices to Wyoming residents and runs a reuse program where lightly used devices and durable medical equipment can be exchanged, bought or donated. Listings are on Wyoming AT4ALL.

Next step: Fill out the device loan request form, or call (307) 766-6187 or email watr@uwyo.edu.

Good to know
  • Reuse items may carry a modest price; check each listing.

Wyoming Telecom Relay Equipment: phone equipment for hearing loss

Wyoming Telecom Relay Equipment

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as income within the family-size limit. Equipment is owned by the recipient.

Next step: Open the website, check the current eligibility and application steps, and ask a hearing health professional to certify your hearing loss if the program requires it.

Good to know
  • Eligibility, income limits and equipment rules change; confirm on the program page before you apply.
  • Some states also cover speech or mobility disabilities; the program page says which.

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (WY)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says Wyoming runs rides directly through an in-house fee-for-service system (information collected Sep 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.
Official page

How to reach them: Call the member services number printed on your Medicaid card.

Statewide

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (WY)

Wyoming Department of Transportation

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. The state passes the money to eligible nonprofits, public agencies and transit operators, which run the vehicles and rides.

Next step: If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging, Center for Independent Living or local transit agency which 5310-funded rides serve your county. If you run a nonprofit or transit service, use this page to see how and when to apply.

Good to know
  • Funds go to eligible organizations, so riders use the services those groups run rather than applying for money themselves.
  • Application rounds, deadlines and forms change; check the page for the current cycle.

Statewide

Wyoming Department of Transportation: rural public transit program (Section 5311)

Wyoming Department of Transportation

The Wyoming Department of Transportation runs the federal Section 5311 program, which funds public transit in towns and rural areas with fewer than 50,000 people. Rural transit systems in this state are paid for in part through this office, and many offer rides that people with disabilities can use.

Next step: Ask the state transit office, or look on the page, for the public transit provider that serves your county, then call that provider and ask about door-to-door rides and how to register.

Good to know
  • This is a state grants office, not a ride booking line.
  • Rural transit hours, fares and service areas are set by each local provider.

Coverage & funding

Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Wyoming)

USDA Rural Development, Casper state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Wyoming Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.

Wyoming Division of Vocational Rehabilitation

The state vocational rehabilitation agency helps people with disabilities prepare for, find and keep work. When equipment is needed for a work goal it can pay for assistive technology and vehicle modifications, and in some states home modifications. Eligibility and the order of service vary by state.

Next step: Call to apply, and ask about assistive technology, vehicle modification and home modification funding before you buy anything.

Good to know
  • Services must connect to an employment goal.
  • Apply and get approval before buying equipment or starting modifications.
  • Some states keep a waitlist or order of selection.

Statewide

iCanConnect in Wyoming: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), Wyoming program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including Wyoming. The Wyoming page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the Wyoming page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.
Call 800-825-4595Official page

National iCanConnect line; the Wyoming page lists the local program contact

Wyoming Department of Education, Vision Outreach: talking-book and braille library service

Wyoming Department of Education, Vision Outreach

Wyoming Department of Education, Vision Outreach in Casper is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving Wyoming. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 307-265-8818, say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

Good to know
  • NLS says this office processes applications and machine lending; the Utah library provides materials and help with BARD downloads.
  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.

Wyoming Institute for Disabilities (WIND)

University of Wyoming

Wyoming Institute for Disabilities (WIND) is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at University of Wyoming. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

Next step: Call the center and ask what community projects, assistive technology help, or referrals it offers for adults and children with developmental disabilities in your area.

  • Services vary by center and funding; many UCEDDs focus on training, research and referral rather than ongoing direct care.

Rights, advocacy & legal help

Statewide

Rocky Mountain ADA Center

The Rocky Mountain ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by Rocky Mountain ADA Center.

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.

Statewide

Wyoming State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Wyoming Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.

Statewide

Wyoming developmental disability waivers: how to apply (statewide)

Wyoming Department of Health, Division of Healthcare Financing, Home and Community-Based Services Section

The Comprehensive and Supports waivers help people with intellectual or developmental disabilities, and some adults with acquired brain injury, get community-based services. You contact the Benefits and Eligibility Specialist for your county, choose a case manager and complete the application. Wyoming 2-1-1 can help with services while you wait.

Next step: Call the Home and Community-Based Services Section and ask for the Benefits and Eligibility Specialist for your county.

Good to know
  • You must be eligible for Wyoming Medicaid and meet level of care rules.
Call 800-510-0280Official page

Toll-free; Cheyenne office 307-777-7531; financial eligibility unit 855-203-2936
