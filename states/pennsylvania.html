Statewide

Pennsylvania Telecommunication Device Distribution Program (TDDP): phone equipment for hearing loss

Pennsylvania Telecommunication Device Distribution Program (TDDP)

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as income not above 200% of the poverty level. Equipment is owned by the recipient.

Next step: Open the website, check the current eligibility and application steps, and ask a hearing health professional to certify your hearing loss if the program requires it.

Good to know
  • Eligibility, income limits and equipment rules change; confirm on the program page before you apply.
  • Some states also cover speech or mobility disabilities; the program page says which.

Pennsylvania Initiative on Assistive Technology, Temple University

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (PA)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says Pennsylvania runs rides directly through county Medical Assistance Transportation Programs (MATP), with Modivcare as the broker in Philadelphia (information collected Sep 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.
Official page

How to reach them: Call the member services number printed on your Medicaid card.

Statewide

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (PA)

Pennsylvania Department of Transportation

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. The state passes the money to eligible nonprofits, public agencies and transit operators, which run the vehicles and rides.

Next step: If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging, Center for Independent Living or local transit agency which 5310-funded rides serve your county. If you run a nonprofit or transit service, use this page to see how and when to apply.

Good to know
  • Funds go to eligible organizations, so riders use the services those groups run rather than applying for money themselves.
  • The link goes to a program document rather than a landing page; forms and deadlines may be elsewhere on the agency site.

Coverage & funding

Statewide

Pennsylvania Office of Vocational Rehabilitation

The state vocational rehabilitation agency helps people with disabilities prepare for, find and keep work. When equipment is needed for a work goal it can pay for assistive technology and vehicle modifications, and in some states home modifications. Eligibility and the order of service vary by state.

Next step: Call to apply, and ask about assistive technology, vehicle modification and home modification funding before you buy anything.

Good to know
  • Services must connect to an employment goal.
  • Apply and get approval before buying equipment or starting modifications.
  • Some states keep a waitlist or order of selection.

iCanConnect in Pennsylvania: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), Pennsylvania program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including Pennsylvania. The Pennsylvania page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the Pennsylvania page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.
Call 800-825-4595Official page

National iCanConnect line; the Pennsylvania page lists the local program contact

LAMP: Library of Accessible Media for Pennsylvanians, Philadelphia: talking-book and braille library service

LAMP: Library of Accessible Media for Pennsylvanians, Philadelphia

LAMP: Library of Accessible Media for Pennsylvanians, Philadelphia in Philadelphia is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving Pennsylvania. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 215-683-3213 (in-state toll-free 800-222-1754), say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.

Temple University

Institute on Disabilities is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at Temple University. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

Pennsylvania Head Injury Program

Pennsylvania Department of Health

Pennsylvania Head Injury Program is run by or through Pennsylvania Department of Health, which the National Association of State Head Injury Administrators lists as the state lead on brain injury. State brain injury programs coordinate services, trust fund or waiver supports, public information and the state advisory council for people with traumatic or acquired brain injury, including veterans and people hurt in crashes, falls or sports. The statewide advocacy group is the Brain Injury Association of Pennsylvania.

Next step: Contact the program and ask about brain injury resource facilitation, any trust fund or waiver services you may qualify for, and local support groups.

Good to know
  • Services and eligibility vary a lot by state; many are funded by trust funds or Medicaid waivers with limited slots.

Rights, advocacy & legal help

Statewide

Mid-Atlantic ADA Center

TransCen, Inc.

The Mid-Atlantic ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by TransCen, Inc..

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.

Statewide

Pennsylvania State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Pennsylvania Department of Aging

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.
