Ramps & home modification

Programs that build, lend or pay for ramps, bathroom changes and other home access work.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Whether you own or rent. If you rent, ask the landlord in writing before any work.
  • Photos of the entrance and a count of the steps, or the height from the ground to the door.
  • What the person uses to get around: wheelchair, scooter, walker or cane.
  • Household size and income, if the program is based on income.

Ask

  • Does the work have to be approved before it starts?
  • Is this a grant, a loan or a free build by volunteers?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Statewide

Persons with Disabilities Program: discounted shared rides in Pennsylvania (ages 18 to 64)

Pennsylvania Department of Transportation

PennDOT’s rural transportation program for persons with disabilities lowers the cost of shared-ride, curb-to-curb trips booked in advance for people with disabilities age 18 through 64 who live or travel outside the fixed-route bus system. The program pays up to 85 percent of the fare for trips that no other funding source covers. It runs in 66 counties.

Next step: Call the shared-ride provider for your county (PennDOT’s transit service map, linked on the page, shows it), say you want to register for the persons with disabilities program, and ask what proof of disability they need. Book rides at least a day ahead.

Good to know
  • Not available in Philadelphia County.
  • Only for trips that no other funding source covers.
  • You pay the rest of the fare. People 65 and older use the senior shared-ride program, which runs in every county.

Statewide

Disability parking placard in Pennsylvania: how to apply

For: Mobility or physical disability

Pennsylvania Department of Transportation (PennDOT)

In Pennsylvania, disability parking placards are issued by the Pennsylvania Department of Transportation (PennDOT). Apply with the Person with Disability Parking Placard Application (MV-145A). Who can certify the disability: a physician, chiropractor, optometrist, podiatrist, physician assistant or certified registered nurse practitioner licensed in Pennsylvania or a neighboring state; a police officer can certify blindness or loss of full use of a leg for a permanent placard.

Next step: Open the agency’s page for the current form and have the medical part completed. Mail it to PennDOT Bureau of Motor Vehicles, P.O. Box 68268, Harrisburg, PA 17106-8268. Some approved health care facilities can hand out temporary placards on the spot.

Good to know
  • A temporary placard lasts up to six months and cannot be extended.
  • There is no fee. A permanent placard lasts five years and can be renewed online.
  • On request, local authorities may post a reserved parking sign on the street near the home of a person with a disability placard or plate.
  • Forms, fees and rules change. Check the agency’s page before you apply.

Statewide

Section 811 Project Rental Assistance in Pennsylvania (PHFA)

Pennsylvania Housing Finance Agency, with the Department of Human Services

Pennsylvania’s Section 811 program gives project-based rental assistance to people with disabilities with extremely low incomes (at or below 30 percent of area median income), ages 18 to 61 at move-in. It is for people who are in an institution but could live in the community with supportive housing, people at risk of going into one, and people in group settings who want to move to the community. Self Determination Housing Partnership (SDHP) manages the waiting list, and Local Lead Agencies screen people in their areas.

Next step: Ask your service coordinator, case manager or the Local Lead Agency for your area to screen you and refer you to the 811 waiting list.

Good to know
  • You cannot apply on your own; you come in through a referral and the waiting list.

The early intervention program in Pennsylvania serves children from birth to age three who have a developmental delay or disability. Parents do not have to wait for a referral, and the evaluation is free.

For: Intellectual or developmental disability

Available in every state

These national lines help wherever you live, including Pennsylvania.

  • 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline: call, text or chat, any time · Call 988 · Website (new tab)
  • 211: one call for local help with food, housing, bills and more · Call 211 · Directory listing (new tab)
  • 711 relay: phone calls for people who are deaf, hard of hearing or have a speech disability · Call 711 · Website (new tab)
  • Social Security: apply for disability benefits (SSDI and SSI) · Call 1-800-772-1213 · Website (new tab)
  • ADA Information Line (U.S. Department of Justice): questions about disability rights law · Call 800-514-0301 · Website (new tab)
  • Rental help: find your local Public Housing Agency (vouchers and public housing) · Call 1-800-955-2232 · Website (new tab)
  • Job Accommodation Network (JAN): free advice on workplace accommodations · Call 800-526-7234 · Website (new tab) · Email
  • EEOC: file a job discrimination charge · Call 1-800-669-4000 · Website (new tab) · Email
  • Medicare equipment problems: repairs, rental supplier duties and replacement after a disaster · Call 800-633-4227 · Website (new tab)
  • State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP): free local Medicare counseling · Call 877-839-2675 · Website (new tab)
  • Work Incentives Planning and Assistance (WIPA): free benefits counseling for people on SSDI or SSI · Call 866-968-7842 · Website (new tab) · Email
  • iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program): national information line · Call 800-825-4595 · Website (new tab)
  • TSA Cares: airport security screening help for travelers with disabilities · Call 855-787-2227 · Government page (new tab)

MIF designs open-source mobility equipment that people can repair, adapt and improve, including the Endurance power chair and the RozRover for children, and it speaks up for users’ right to repair. MIF is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit.
