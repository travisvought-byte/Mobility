Ramps & home modification

Programs that build, lend or pay for ramps, bathroom changes and other home access work.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Whether you own or rent. If you rent, ask the landlord in writing before any work.
  • Photos of the entrance and a count of the steps, or the height from the ground to the door.
  • What the person uses to get around: wheelchair, scooter, walker or cane.
  • Household size and income, if the program is based on income.

Ask

  • Does the work have to be approved before it starts?
  • Is this a grant, a loan or a free build by volunteers?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Disability parking placard in Texas: how to apply

For: Mobility or physical disability

Texas Department of Motor Vehicles (through county tax assessor-collectors)

In Texas, disability parking placards are issued by the Texas Department of Motor Vehicles (through county tax assessor-collectors). Apply with the Application for Persons with Disabilities Parking Placard and/or License Plate (VTR-214). Who can certify the disability: a physician, podiatrist, optometrist, or qualifying physician assistant or advanced practice nurse licensed in Texas, Arkansas, Louisiana, New Mexico or Oklahoma (or practicing at a Texas military installation or VA facility); the signature must be notarized unless you attach an original prescription.

Next step: Open the agency’s page for the current form and have the medical part completed. Submit it in person or by mail to the county tax assessor-collector’s office where you live (or, for a placard, where you are getting medical treatment), with a copy of your photo ID.

Good to know
  • A permanent (blue) placard is free; a temporary (red) placard costs $5.
  • Vehicles showing a disability placard or plate do not pay state or local government parking meter fees; federal meters and airport parking are not covered.
  • Forms, fees and rules change. Check the agency’s page before you apply.

How to reach them: Apply through the agency’s page, which lists its offices and how to reach them.

Texas Department of Transportation: rural public transit program (Section 5311)

Texas Department of Transportation

The Texas Department of Transportation runs the federal Section 5311 program, which funds public transit in towns and rural areas with fewer than 50,000 people. Rural transit systems in this state are paid for in part through this office, and many offer rides that people with disabilities can use.

Next step: Ask the state transit office, or look on the page, for the public transit provider that serves your county, then call that provider and ask about door-to-door rides and how to register.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Your Social Security number and a photo ID.
  • Names, addresses and dates for your doctors, clinics and hospitals, and a list of your medicines.
  • Recent pay stubs, benefit letters and bank statements.

Ask

  • Which program fits my situation, and can I apply for more than one?
  • What is the deadline to appeal, and where do I send the appeal?
  • Will working or saving money change my benefits?
  • Who can help me fill out the forms at no cost?

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Texas)

USDA Rural Development, Temple state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Texas Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.

Section 811 Project Rental Assistance in Texas (TDHCA)

Texas Department of Housing and Community Affairs

Texas’s Section 811 program gives project-based rental assistance, linked with long-term services, to extremely low-income people with disabilities. It serves people with disabilities living in institutions, people with serious mental illness, and youth with disabilities leaving foster care. Early intervention for babies and toddlers (birth to age 3)

For: Children, youth and families

Early Childhood Intervention Services; Family Health Services Department

The early intervention program in Texas serves children from birth to age three who have a developmental delay or disability. Parents do not have to wait for a referral, and the evaluation is free.

Next step: Call the referral line. Say you are concerned about your child’s development and want an evaluation under Part C of IDEA. Write down the name of each person you speak with and the date.

Good to know
  • For children under age three. If your child is three or older, ask your school district for a special education evaluation, which is also free.
  • The evaluation and service coordination are free. Depending on state policy, some services may have a sliding-scale fee. A child cannot be denied services because the family cannot pay.
  • For an eligible child, the steps from referral to a written service plan (an Individualized Family Service Plan) should take 45 days.
  • This is the office the ECTA Center, a technical assistance center funded by the U.S. Department of Education, lists for Texas under Part C of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA). The points here about referral, cost and timing come from the Center for Parent Information and Resources.
