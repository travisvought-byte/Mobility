Ramps & home modification

Programs that build, lend or pay for ramps, bathroom changes and other home access work.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Whether you own or rent. If you rent, ask the landlord in writing before any work.
  • Photos of the entrance and a count of the steps, or the height from the ground to the door.
  • What the person uses to get around: wheelchair, scooter, walker or cane.
  • Household size and income, if the program is based on income.

Ask

  • Does the work have to be approved before it starts?
  • Is this a grant, a loan or a free build by volunteers?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Equipment, repair & assistive technology

Try, borrow, repair, finance or reuse wheelchairs and assistive technology.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • The make, model and serial number of the equipment, if you have them.
  • Who paid for it and when: Medicare, Medicaid, the VA, private insurance or you.
  • The name of the supplier or dealer, and any warranty papers.
  • What is wrong, when it started and what it keeps you from doing.

Ask

  • Can I borrow or try equipment while I wait?
  • Is there a cost, and can it be reduced or waived?
  • How long is the wait, and who do I call to check on it?
  • Do you serve my county?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Statewide

Massachusetts transit authorities: find paratransit for your town

Massachusetts Department of Transportation

The MBTA and 15 Regional Transit Authorities run bus and paratransit service across Massachusetts. ADA paratransit, sometimes called dial-a-ride or demand-response, is a shared ride for people whose disability sometimes or always keeps them from using a fixed-route bus, in the area the buses cover. Some authorities offer paratransit beyond what federal law requires. The state’s page shows which authority serves each city and town, with its phone number.

Next step: Find your town on the page, then call that transit authority and ask how to apply for paratransit, whether it offers rides beyond ADA paratransit, and how far ahead to book.

Good to know
  • You must apply and be found eligible before you can book paratransit rides.
  • What is offered differs by transit authority; ask yours what applies.

Disability parking placard in Massachusetts: how to apply

For: Mobility or physical disability

Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles (RMV), Medical Affairs

In Massachusetts, disability parking placards are issued by the Massachusetts Registry of Motor Vehicles (RMV), Medical Affairs. Apply with the Application for Disabled Parking Placard/Plate (MAB100). Who can certify the disability: a Massachusetts-licensed medical doctor, osteopath, chiropractor, podiatrist, registered nurse or physician assistant, or an optometrist for legal blindness only.

Next step: Open the agency’s page for the current form and have the medical part completed. Mail the original application to Registry of Motor Vehicles, Attn: Medical Affairs, PO Box 55889, Boston, MA 02205-5889, within 30 days of your provider’s signature. RMV service centers do not process these applications. Allow at least 30 business days.

Good to know
  • A temporary placard is for a disability expected to last at least two months and less than 24 months.
  • There is no fee for a placard; registration fees apply to disability plates.
  • A Certificate of Blindness can take the place of the provider’s certification.
  • Forms, fees and rules change. Check the agency’s page before you apply.

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Massachusetts)

USDA Rural Development, Amherst, MA state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Massachusetts Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.

For: Blind or low vision, Older adults

Massachusetts Commission for the Blind (Older Independent Blind program)

The Massachusetts Commission for the Blind runs the Older Independent Blind program with federal funds. It serves Massachusetts residents 55 and older who are legally blind, whose blindness substantially limits how well they can manage at home or in the community, and for whom independent living services would make a real difference.
