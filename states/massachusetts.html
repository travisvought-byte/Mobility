Massachusetts State AT Program: portable ramp loans

Massachusetts Assistive Technology Act program

The state Assistive Technology Act program lends portable wheelchair ramps, often free, for temporary visits or to help someone leave a hospital or rehab facility. MassMATCH lends portable ramps for temporary visits and discharge.

Next step: Contact your state AT program and ask what ramp sizes are available.

Equipment, repair & assistive technology

Statewide

Massachusetts Maximizing Assistive Technology in Consumers' Hands (MATCH)

This is the state Assistive Technology Act program, funded by the federal Administration for Community Living. It lets you try equipment before you buy (device demonstration), borrow it short term (device loan), get refurbished used equipment at low or no cost (reuse), and in many states apply for a low-interest financial loan. It serves people of all ages with any disability.

Next step: Call and ask about borrowing or trying a device, the reuse or exchange program, and any financial loan for equipment you need.

Good to know
  • Equipment and services offered vary by state and by program funding.

Massachusetts Equipment Distribution Program (MassEDP): phone equipment for hearing loss

Massachusetts Equipment Distribution Program (MassEDP)

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as hearing loss certified by a medical professional. Equipment is loaned.

Next step: Open the website, check the current eligibility and application steps, and ask a hearing health professional to certify your hearing loss if the program requires it.

Good to know

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (MA)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says Massachusetts uses regional brokers hired by the State: Montachusett Regional Transit Authority and Greater Attleboro and Taunton Regional Transit Authority (information collected Dec 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.
Official page

How to reach them: Call the member services number printed on your Medicaid card.

Statewide

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (MA)

Massachusetts Community Transit Grant Program

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. The state passes the money to eligible nonprofits, public agencies and transit operators, which run the vehicles and rides.

Next step: If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging, Center for Independent Living or local transit agency which 5310-funded rides serve your county. If you run a nonprofit or transit service, use this page to see how and when to apply.

Good to know
  • Funds go to eligible organizations, so riders use the services those groups run rather than applying for money themselves.
  • Application rounds, deadlines and forms change; check the page for the current cycle.

Coverage & funding

Statewide

Massachusetts Rehabilitation Commission

The state vocational rehabilitation agency helps people with disabilities prepare for, find and keep work. When equipment is needed for a work goal it can pay for assistive technology and vehicle modifications, and in some states home modifications. Eligibility and the order of service vary by state.

Next step: Call to apply, and ask about assistive technology, vehicle modification and home modification funding before you buy anything.

Good to know
  • Services must connect to an employment goal.
  • Apply and get approval before buying equipment or starting modifications.
  • Some states keep a waitlist or order of selection.

Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Massachusetts)

USDA Rural Development, Amherst, MA state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Massachusetts Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.
Call (413) 253-4300Official page

State office main line from the USDA Rural Development state office directory; confirm before calling. This office serves more than one state

Statewide

Home Modification Loan Program (MassAbility)

MassAbility

Deferred, zero-percent loans for accessibility changes to a home where a person with a disability or an older adult lives. Loans run from $1,000 to $50,000, with a $30,000 limit for mobile homes.

Next step: Contact MassAbility and ask about the Home Modification Loan Program and how to apply.

Good to know
  • Loan, not a grant; terms are set by the program.
  • Limit is $30,000 for a mobile home.

iCanConnect in Massachusetts: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), Massachusetts program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including Massachusetts. The Massachusetts page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the Massachusetts page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.
Call 800-825-4595Official page

National iCanConnect line; the Massachusetts page lists the local program contact

Massachusetts Commission for the Blind: state agency for blind and low-vision residents

Massachusetts Commission for the Blind

Massachusetts Commission for the Blind is Massachusetts's state agency for people who are blind or visually impaired. The U.S. Department of Education's Rehabilitation Services Administration lists it as a blind-type vocational rehabilitation agency, so it is a state route to rehabilitation services for blind and low-vision residents of Massachusetts.

Next step: Call the number listed, say you are blind or have low vision, and ask how to apply, what services the agency offers, and who your first contact will be. If you use a relay service, use the TTY number or the agency website.

Good to know
  • Listing comes from a federal directory; confirm eligibility, services and intake steps with the agency.
  • Some states split services between a blind agency and a general vocational rehabilitation agency; ask which one handles your situation.

Perkins Library: talking-book and braille library service

Perkins Library

Perkins Library in Watertown is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving the Massachusetts counties NLS lists for it, and braille readers in Maine, New Hampshire, Rhode Island and Vermont. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 617-972-7240 (in-state toll-free 800-852-3133), say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

Good to know

  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.

Rights, advocacy & legal help

Statewide

Massachusetts State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Massachusetts Executive Office of Elder Affairs

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.
Call 617-222-7491Official page

Statewide

New England ADA Center

Institute for Human Centered Design

The New England ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by Institute for Human Centered Design.

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.
