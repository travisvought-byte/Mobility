Equipment, repair & assistive technology

Statewide

STAR Program (System of Technology to Achieve Results)

Minnesota Department of Administration

Minnesota's assistive technology program. It lends medical equipment and assistive technology at no cost for a set loan period, runs demonstrations and reuse services, and serves people of all ages. No proof of disability is required.

Next step: Call or email STAR to borrow a device or find used equipment.

Good to know
  • Loans are time-limited; short-term loans are 45 days or less.

Minnesota Telephone Equipment Distribution Program: phone equipment for hearing loss

Minnesota Telephone Equipment Distribution Program

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as income within the family-size limit. Equipment is loaned as long as needed.

Next step: Open the website, check the current eligibility and application steps, and ask a hearing health professional to certify your hearing loss if the program requires it.

Good to know
  • Eligibility, income limits and equipment rules change; confirm on the program page before you apply.
  • Some states also cover speech or mobility disabilities; the program page says which.

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (MN)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says Minnesota runs rides directly and through regional brokers that work with managed care organizations (information collected Sep 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.
Official page

How to reach them: Call the member services number printed on your Medicaid card.

Statewide

Minnesota Department of Transportation: rural public transit program (Section 5311)

Minnesota Department of Transportation

The Minnesota Department of Transportation runs the federal Section 5311 program, which funds public transit in towns and rural areas with fewer than 50,000 people. Rural transit systems in this state are paid for in part through this office, and many offer rides that people with disabilities can use.

Next step: Ask the state transit office, or look on the page, for the public transit provider that serves your county, then call that provider and ask about door-to-door rides and how to register.

Good to know
  • This is a state grants office, not a ride booking line.
  • Rural transit hours, fares and service areas are set by each local provider.

Statewide

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (MN)

Minnesota Department of Transportation

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. The state passes the money to eligible nonprofits, public agencies and transit operators, which run the vehicles and rides.

Next step: If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging, Center for Independent Living or local transit agency which 5310-funded rides serve your county. If you run a nonprofit or transit service, use this page to see how and when to apply.

Good to know
  • Funds go to eligible organizations, so riders use the services those groups run rather than applying for money themselves.
  • Application rounds, deadlines and forms change; check the page for the current cycle.

Statewide

Minnesota Vocational Rehabilitation Services

The state vocational rehabilitation agency helps people with disabilities prepare for, find and keep work. When equipment is needed for a work goal it can pay for assistive technology and vehicle modifications, and in some states home modifications. Eligibility and the order of service vary by state.

Next step: Call to apply, and ask about assistive technology, vehicle modification and home modification funding before you buy anything.

Good to know
  • Services must connect to an employment goal.
  • Apply and get approval before buying equipment or starting modifications.
  • Some states keep a waitlist or order of selection.

Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Minnesota)

USDA Rural Development, St. Paul state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Minnesota Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.
Call (651) 602-7800Official page

Statewide

iCanConnect in Minnesota: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), Minnesota program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including Minnesota. The Minnesota page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the Minnesota page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.
Call 800-825-4595Official page

National iCanConnect line; the Minnesota page lists the local program contact

Statewide

Minnesota Braille and Talking Book Library: talking-book and braille library service

Minnesota Braille and Talking Book Library

Minnesota Braille and Talking Book Library in Minneapolis is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving Minnesota. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 800-722-0550 or use the library website, say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

Good to know
  • Braille readers in Minnesota are served by the Utah library, per NLS.
  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.
Call 800-722-0550Official page

Main line as listed in the Library of Congress NLS Find a Library directory

Minnesota State Services for the Blind: state agency for blind and low-vision residents

Minnesota State Services for the Blind

Minnesota State Services for the Blind is Minnesota's state agency for people who are blind or visually impaired. The U.S. Department of Education's Rehabilitation Services Administration lists it as a blind-type vocational rehabilitation agency, so it is a state route to rehabilitation services for blind and low-vision residents of Minnesota.

Next step: Call the number listed, say you are blind or have low vision, and ask how to apply, what services the agency offers, and who your first contact will be. If you use a relay service, use the TTY number or the agency website.

Good to know
  • Listing comes from a federal directory; confirm eligibility, services and intake steps with the agency.
  • Some states split services between a blind agency and a general vocational rehabilitation agency; ask which one handles your situation.

Institute on Community Integration

University of Minnesota

Institute on Community Integration is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at University of Minnesota. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

Next step: Call the center and ask what community projects, assistive technology help, or referrals it offers for adults and children with developmental disabilities in your area.

Good to know
  • Services vary by center and funding; many UCEDDs focus on training, research and referral rather than ongoing direct care.

Minnesota Brain Injury Program and Waiver

Minnesota Department of Human Services, Disability Services Division

Minnesota Brain Injury Program and Waiver is run by or through Minnesota Department of Human Services, Disability Services Division, which the National Association of State Head Injury Administrators lists as the state lead on brain injury. State brain injury programs coordinate services, trust fund or waiver supports, public information and the state advisory council for people with traumatic or acquired brain injury, including veterans and people hurt in crashes, falls or sports. Rights, advocacy & legal help

Statewide

Great Lakes ADA Center

University of Illinois Chicago, Institute on Disability and Human Development

The Great Lakes ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by University of Illinois Chicago, Institute on Disability and Human Development.

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.

Statewide

Minnesota State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Minnesota Office of Ombudsman for Long-Term Care

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.

Minnesota Developmental Disabilities Waiver: apply through your county or tribal nation (statewide)

Minnesota Department of Human Services

Minnesota runs its Developmental Disabilities (DD) Waiver through county and tribal nation "lead agencies." You apply at the county social services office or tribal nation where you live, and a screening decides eligibility. The state lists a DD waiver contact for each lead agency.

Next step: Call Disability Hub MN or your county social services office and ask how to apply for the DD waiver.

Good to know
  • People with a developmental disability or related condition who need the level of care of an intermediate care facility.
