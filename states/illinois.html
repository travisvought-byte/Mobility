Equipment, repair & assistive technology

Statewide

Illinois Assistive Technology Program

This is the state Assistive Technology Act program, funded by the federal Administration for Community Living. It lets you try equipment before you buy (device demonstration), borrow it short term (device loan), get refurbished used equipment at low or no cost (reuse), and in many states apply for a low-interest financial loan. It serves people of all ages with any disability.

Next step: Call and ask about borrowing or trying a device, the reuse or exchange program, and any financial loan for equipment you need.

Good to know
  • Equipment and services offered vary by state and by program funding.

Statewide

Illinois Telecommunications Access Corporation (ITAC): phone equipment for hearing loss

Illinois Telecommunications Access Corporation (ITAC)

This state program provides specialized telephone equipment, such as amplified or captioned phones, to residents with hearing loss. The Hearing Loss Association of America list gives eligibility as Illinois resident with landline or cellular service whose medical professional confirms they cannot use a standard phone. Equipment is vouchers for designated equipment, plus loaned equipment.

Next step: Open the website, check the current eligibility and application steps, and ask a hearing health professional to certify your hearing loss if the program requires it.

Good to know
  • Eligibility, income limits and equipment rules change; confirm on the program page before you apply.
  • Some states also cover speech or mobility disabilities; the program page says which.

Rides & transportation

Statewide

Illinois Department of Transportation: rural public transit program (Section 5311)

Illinois Department of Transportation

The Illinois Department of Transportation runs the federal Section 5311 program, which funds public transit in towns and rural areas with fewer than 50,000 people. Rural transit systems in this state are paid for in part through this office, and many offer rides that people with disabilities can use.

Next step: Ask the state transit office, or look on the page, for the public transit provider that serves your county, then call that provider and ask about door-to-door rides and how to register.

Good to know
  • This is a state grants office, not a ride booking line.
  • Rural transit hours, fares and service areas are set by each local provider.

Statewide

Medicaid rides to medical appointments (IL)

State Medicaid agency and its ride contractors

Medicaid pays for rides to covered medical appointments for members who have no other way to get there. A 2024 report from the federal transportation coordination council says Illinois runs rides directly and through regional brokers that work with managed care plans; a program called NETSPAP handles prior authorization but does not arrange rides (information collected Dec 2024).

Next step: Call the member services number on your Medicaid card and ask how to schedule a ride, how far ahead to book, and whether wheelchair-accessible vehicles are available.

Good to know
  • Rides are for Medicaid-covered services; who runs scheduling depends on your health plan and county.
  • The contractors named were current in 2024 and change often, so confirm with your plan or the state Medicaid office.
Official page

How to reach them: Call the member services number printed on your Medicaid card.

Statewide

Section 5310 transportation funding for older adults and people with disabilities (IL)

Illinois Department of Transportation

The state's page for the federal Section 5310 program, which pays for transportation for older adults and people with disabilities when other service is unavailable, insufficient or inappropriate. The state passes the money to eligible nonprofits, public agencies and transit operators, which run the vehicles and rides.

Next step: If you need a ride, ask your Area Agency on Aging, Center for Independent Living or local transit agency which 5310-funded rides serve your county. If you run a nonprofit or transit service, use this page to see how and when to apply.

Good to know
  • Funds go to eligible organizations, so riders use the services those groups run rather than applying for money themselves.
  • The link goes to a program document rather than a landing page; forms and deadlines may be elsewhere on the agency site.

Statewide

USDA Rural Development home repair loans and grants (Illinois)

USDA Rural Development, Champaign state office

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Repairs and improvements can sometimes include accessibility changes such as ramps or bathroom work, so ask the local office what qualifies.

Next step: Call the Illinois Rural Development state office, ask for Single Family Housing, and ask which local office takes 504 repair applications for your county.

Good to know
  • Homeowners only; the home must be in an eligible rural area and household income must be low or very low for the county.
  • Grants are for homeowners age 62 and older with very low income; funding is limited each federal fiscal year.
Call 217-403-6200Official page

Statewide

iCanConnect in Illinois: free phone and internet equipment for people who are deaf-blind

iCanConnect (National Deaf-Blind Equipment Distribution Program), Illinois program

iCanConnect provides free distance communication technology and training to people with both significant hearing and vision loss. The FCC administers it and a local partner carries it out in each state, including Illinois. The Illinois page on iCanConnect.org gives the local contact.

Next step: Open the Illinois page for the local program's phone, email and mailing instructions, or call the national iCanConnect line and ask to be connected. Ask who can verify your hearing and vision loss and what income documents are needed.

Good to know
  • For low-income people who are deaf-blind (combined hearing and vision loss); programs verify disability and income.
  • Contact details and the local partner can change; use the state page for the current contact.
Call 800-825-4595Official page

National iCanConnect line; the Illinois page lists the local program contact

Statewide

Illinois State Library Talking Book and Braille Service: talking-book and braille library service

Illinois State Library Talking Book and Braille Service

Illinois State Library Talking Book and Braille Service in Springfield is part of the Library of Congress National Library Service for the Blind and Print Disabled (NLS) network. The NLS Find a Library directory lists it as a regional library serving all of Illinois except Chicago. Network libraries supply audio and braille reading materials to people who cannot read standard print.

Next step: Call 217-785-0022 (in-state toll-free 800-426-0709), say you want to apply for talking-book service, and ask about eligibility, how to get a playback device and how to download books.

Good to know
  • Chicago residents are served by the Chicago Public Library center.
  • The network serves people who are blind or print disabled; ask the library what proof of eligibility it needs.
  • Service areas and hours change; confirm with the library before you rely on this listing.
Call 217-785-0022Official page

Institute on Disability and Human Development

University of Illinois Chicago

Institute on Disability and Human Development is the federally funded University Center for Excellence in Developmental Disabilities (UCEDD) at University of Illinois Chicago. UCEDDs are required to serve every state and work with people with developmental disabilities, their families, and community agencies through training, information and referral, technical assistance, research and direct community projects such as assistive technology, transition, and employment support.

Illinois Brain Injury Services

Illinois Department of Human Services, Division of Rehabilitation Services

Illinois Brain Injury Services is run by or through Illinois Department of Human Services, Division of Rehabilitation Services, which the National Association of State Head Injury Administrators lists as the state lead on brain injury. State brain injury programs coordinate services, trust fund or waiver supports, public information and the state advisory council for people with traumatic or acquired brain injury, including veterans and people hurt in crashes, falls or sports. The statewide advocacy group is the Brain Injury Association of Illinois.

Next step: Contact the program and ask about brain injury resource facilitation, any trust fund or waiver services you may qualify for, and local support groups.

Good to know
  • Services and eligibility vary a lot by state; many are funded by trust funds or Medicaid waivers with limited slots.

Rights, advocacy & legal help

Statewide

Great Lakes ADA Center

University of Illinois Chicago, Institute on Disability and Human Development

The Great Lakes ADA Center is the regional ADA Center for this state in the ADA National Network. It answers questions about the Americans with Disabilities Act, including access to buildings, programs, transportation and employment, and it gives guidance and training. It is hosted by University of Illinois Chicago, Institute on Disability and Human Development.

Next step: Call or use the website and ask your ADA question; the center can explain the law and your options, but it does not take legal cases.

Good to know
  • ADA Centers give information and guidance; they are not law firms and do not enforce the ADA.
  • The number serves your whole region, not only this state.

Free civil legal help for people with low income. Land of Lincoln Legal Aid serves 65 central and southern Illinois counties (877-342-7891). Statewide

Illinois State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Illinois Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities. They take complaints about care, rights, discharge and billing and work to resolve them at the resident's direction. Family members, friends and visiting volunteers can call too.

Next step: Call the state ombudsman office and ask to be connected to the local ombudsman for the facility where the resident lives.

Good to know
  • The ombudsman works for the resident and cannot take sides with the facility or family; services are free and confidential.
