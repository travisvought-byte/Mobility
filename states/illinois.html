Ramps & home modification

Programs that build, lend or pay for ramps, bathroom changes and other home access work.

Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

  • Whether you own or rent. If you rent, ask the landlord in writing before any work.
  • Photos of the entrance and a count of the steps, or the height from the ground to the door.
  • What the person uses to get around: wheelchair, scooter, walker or cane.
  • Household size and income, if the program is based on income.

Ask

  • Does the work have to be approved before it starts?
  • Is this a grant, a loan or a free build by volunteers?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?
  • Is there a waiting list, and how long is it?

These are general reminders. Each program sets its own rules, so ask what it needs.

Disability parking placard in Illinois: how to apply

For: Mobility or physical disability

Illinois Secretary of State

In Illinois, disability parking placards are issued by the Illinois Secretary of State. Apply with the Persons with Disabilities Certification for Parking Placard or License Plates (VSD 62). Who can certify the disability: a licensed medical professional named on the form, such as a physician, physician assistant or advanced practice nurse.

Next step: Open the agency’s page for the current form and have the medical part completed. For a permanent placard, you or your doctor mail the form to the Secretary of State’s Persons with Disabilities Placard Unit in Springfield (address on the form); allow 45 to 60 days. For a temporary placard, take the form to any Secretary of State DMV.

Good to know
  • A temporary placard is valid for up to six months.
  • Placards are free to eligible Illinois residents.
  • A meter-exempt placard is available for some drivers; the form explains who qualifies.
  • Forms, fees and rules change. Check the agency’s page before you apply.

The USDA Section 504 Home Repair program gives loans to low-income and very-low-income homeowners in eligible rural areas to repair, improve or modernize their homes, and grants to very-low-income homeowners age 62 and older to remove health and safety hazards. Before you call: what to have ready and what to ask

Have ready

For: Children, youth and families

Department of Human Services; Bureau of Early Intervention

The early intervention program in Illinois serves children from birth to age three who have a developmental delay or disability. Parents do not have to wait for a referral, and the evaluation is free.

  • This is the office the ECTA Center, a technical assistance center funded by the U.S. Department of Education, lists for Illinois under Part C of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA). Illinois Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman

Every state has an Office of the State Long-Term Care Ombudsman. Ombudsmen are free, confidential advocates for people living in nursing homes, assisted living and other care facilities.
