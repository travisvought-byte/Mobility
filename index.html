ADVOCACY

Right to repair. Right to modify.

Right to repair means owners and independent technicians can get the parts, tools, manuals and software needed to fix a wheelchair or scooter. Right to modify means you can adjust, adapt and improve your own equipment to fit your body and your life.

Several states, including Colorado, Washington, Oregon and Nevada, have enacted wheelchair repair laws, and a federal Wheelchair Right to Repair Act (H.R. 5039) has been introduced in Congress. Scope and start dates differ by state, so check yours.

FEATURED ORGANIZATION

Mobility Independence Foundation

MIF designs open-source mobility equipment that people can repair, adapt and improve, including the Endurance power chair and the RozRover for children, and it speaks up for users’ right to repair. MIF is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit.

If you would like to support that work, you can donate to MIF.

ALSO FEATURED · WASHINGTON

Light Upon Light

Light Upon Light is a Washington nonprofit led by a differently abled founder. It works through advocacy, accessibility, education and equality to build a community where dignity is honored, belonging is expected and differences are celebrated.

If you live in Washington, or want disability-inclusion education for your own community, start with their website. You can also donate or volunteer there.
