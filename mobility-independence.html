Right to repair and right to modify

Right to repair: owners and independent repair providers can get the parts, tools, documentation and software needed to fix their equipment, without waiting weeks or months for one authorized dealer.

Right to modify: people can adjust, adapt and improve their own equipment, with appropriate technical support. Open-source designs like those MIF develops are built to be repaired and modified locally.

Where things stand

  • Colorado passed the first powered wheelchair repair law in 2022. Washington, Oregon and Nevada have since enacted wheelchair repair laws, several taking effect January 1, 2026.
  • A federal Wheelchair Right to Repair Act (H.R. 5039) was introduced in the House on August 26, 2025.
  • Bills have been proposed in other states. Each law differs in which devices it covers and who enforces it, so read your own state’s version.

Repair it. Adapt it. Keep it useful.

  • Repair: access to parts, service information, diagnostics and a choice of repair provider.
  • Adapt: equipment that can be customized to a person’s changing needs, with appropriate technical support.
  • Reuse: repairable equipment that can continue helping someone rather than being discarded.

These are related goals. A repair campaign may address only some of them; its scope matters.
