Disability help outside the United States

Mobility Talk began as a guide for every U.S. state. It is going global, one country at a time. The first countries are Australia, Canada, India, Ireland, Jamaica, Kenya, Mexico, New Zealand, Nigeria, the Philippines, South Africa and the United Kingdom. Each page starts with the emergency number and a crisis line, then lists where to start, disability payments, equipment and home grants, and who handles discrimination, all checked against official pages.

Help that works in any country

Find A Helpline

ThroughLine

A free directory of crisis and support lines in more than 130 countries. Pick your country and what you want to talk about, and it shows lines you can call, text or chat with.

Next step: Choose your country on the site. Most lines are free and many are open any hour.

Be My Eyes

A free app for people who are blind or have low vision. A sighted volunteer looks through your phone's camera and tells you what they see, or an AI describes a photo. The company says it works in about 150 countries and 180 languages.

Next step: Download the app, choose your language, and start a video call or send a photo. It is free.

U.S. citizens abroad: emergency help

U.S. Department of State

For Americans living or traveling outside the U.S. The State Department helps U.S. citizens abroad with emergencies, including crime, arrest and lost passports.

Next step: Contact the nearest U.S. embassy, or call any hour. From the U.S. and Canada, call 1-888-407-4747.
