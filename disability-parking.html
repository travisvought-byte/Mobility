Disability parking placards and plates

Need a disability parking placard or plates? Who qualifies

Each state runs its own placard program and decides who qualifies. Federal guidelines give states a common model for a person whose ability to walk is limited. [1]

  • Under the federal model, a licensed physician decides whether you qualify. [1]
  • The model covers a person who cannot walk 200 feet without stopping to rest, or who cannot walk without a brace, cane, crutch, another person, a prosthetic device, a wheelchair or another assistive device. [1]
  • It also covers a person who uses portable oxygen, has lung disease that meets set breathing or blood-oxygen measures, has a heart condition rated Class III or Class IV by the American Heart Association, or is severely limited in walking by an arthritic, neurological or orthopedic condition. [1]
  • States write their own definitions and forms, so the state page in the list below has the final word on who qualifies where you live. [1]

Placard or plates

Most states offer two things: a placard you hang in the vehicle you are riding in, and special license plates for a vehicle registered to you. [1][2]

  • Under the federal model, special plates go on a vehicle registered in the name of the person with the disability. States can also issue plates to an organization’s vehicle that carries people with disabilities. [1]
  • A placard hangs from the rearview mirror so it can be seen from the front and back of the vehicle, or goes on the dashboard if there is no mirror. Every placard shows its expiration date. [1]
  • A person who does not have special plates can ask for one additional placard. [1]
  • Only plates and placards that show the International Symbol of Access are recognized under the federal model. [1]
  • In California, placards and plates give the same parking privileges. Plates stay on the vehicle; a placard moves with you from one vehicle to another. [2]
  • A placard belongs to the person, not the car. Oregon says its permit can be used in any vehicle the person with the disability rides in, and California says you are the only person who can use your placard or plates. [3][2]
  • Nevada says it is illegal to park in an accessible space with a placard or plates unless the person they were issued to is there. [4]
  • Virginia gives each holder an ID card for the placard, to carry and show to law enforcement when asked. [5]
  • In New York, the DMV issues the plates, but parking permits come from your city, town or village, not the DMV. [6]

Temporary or long-term

States issue temporary placards for a disability expected to last a limited time, and longer-term placards for one that will not go away. [1][4]

  • Under the federal model, a temporary placard lasts as long as the physician sets, up to six months, and the certification has to say how long. [1]
  • California issues temporary placards for up to 180 days, and Oregon issues temporary permits for up to six months. [2][3]
  • Nevada has three kinds: a temporary placard for up to six months, a moderate placard for up to two years and a permanent placard for ten years. [4]

What your medical provider certifies

The application has a part your medical provider fills out to certify that you meet the state’s definition. [1][7]

  • The federal model asks for a licensed physician’s certification on the first application for plates or a placard. [1]
  • Many states accept other licensed providers too. California accepts a physician, surgeon, physician assistant, nurse practitioner or certified nurse midwife, and a chiropractor for loss of use of the lower extremities. [2]
  • Oregon accepts a licensed physician, physician associate, nurse practitioner or optometrist. [3]
  • For a temporary placard, the provider says how long the condition is expected to last. [1]
  • Kansas uses one certification of disability form for its placard, plates and decal. [7]

Renewing

Placards expire. The federal model has states issue placards and renew them from time to time, and the date is printed on each one. [1]

  • California’s permanent placard is good for two years and expires on June 30 of odd-numbered years. Once it is issued, renewing it does not need a new certification from your medical professional. [2]
  • In Oregon, the medical certification expires at the same time as the permit. [3]

Using it in another state

Federal guidelines ask every state to honor placards and plates from other states and countries for parking in spaces reserved for people with disabilities. [1]

  • That covers removable placards, temporary placards and special license plates from other states and countries. [1]
  • Nevada, Utah and Wisconsin say on their own pages that a valid placard or plate from another state can be used there. Utah includes visitors from Canada and Mexico. [4][8][9]
  • The parking rules where you park still apply. Hawaii honors other states’ permits in reserved accessible spaces, but parking fees still apply. [10]
  • If you move, get a placard from your new state. Nevada requires one once you become a resident. [4]
  • California issues a travel placard to visitors for up to 90 days, and a 30-day travel placard to residents who have a permanent placard. [2]
  • A New York City parking permit is not valid outside the city. [6]

Disabled veteran plates

Many states offer disabled veteran license plates. They are a separate program, and a disabled veteran plate does not always let you park in accessible spaces. [3][11]

  • In Oregon, Disabled Veteran license plates do not qualify for parking in a disabled person parking space. [3]
  • Kansas offers a disabled veteran plate to veterans with a service-connected disability rated at least 50 percent, the loss or loss of use of the feet or hands, or a permanent visual impairment of both eyes. [11]
  • In Kansas, only veterans who also meet the state’s disability parking rules get the version with the wheelchair emblem. [11]
  • Washington has a separate disabled veteran parking privilege for veterans with a qualifying discharge and a disability rating of 70 percent or more who use a service animal. It lets them park free in time-limited street spaces, including metered ones, and in disabled parking spaces, and is renewed every five years. [12]

Free parking, and who sets the rules

The federal placard model is guidance to states, so each state and city decides extra privileges such as free or longer parking at meters. The Americans with Disabilities Act covers the accessible spaces themselves. [1][13]

  • Virginia lets placard holders park up to four hours at metered or time-limited spaces without paying, unless the locality prohibits it. [5]
  • Virginia also says a placard or plates do not let you ignore state or local parking rules. [5]
  • Hawaii charges parking fees to placard holders, unless they hold a separate exemption permit for people with a driver’s license who physically cannot reach or operate a parking meter or pay station. [10]
  • Under the ADA, a business or a state or local government that restripes a parking lot must provide the accessible spaces the 2010 ADA Standards require. [13]
  • An accessible car space needs an access aisle at least 60 inches wide. At least one of every six accessible spaces, and always at least one, must be van accessible: 132 inches wide with a 60-inch aisle, or 96 inches wide with a 96-inch aisle. [13]
  • Accessible spaces go on the shortest accessible route to an accessible entrance, with a sign showing the international symbol of accessibility. Van spaces are also signed van accessible. [13]
  • State or local laws may set the color and the way spaces and access aisles are painted. [13]

Quick answers

Who qualifies for a disability parking placard?

Each state decides, following a federal model. Generally that is a person a licensed provider certifies cannot walk 200 feet without stopping to rest, needs a cane, crutch, wheelchair or other device to walk, uses portable oxygen, or has serious lung or heart disease or a severe arthritic, neurological or orthopedic condition that limits walking. [1]

Can I use my placard in another state?

Generally yes. Federal guidelines ask states to honor placards and plates from other states and countries in reserved accessible spaces, but the parking rules and fees where you park still apply. [1][10]

Do I get free parking at meters?

It depends on the state and city. Virginia allows up to four hours at meters and time-limited spaces without paying unless the locality prohibits it, while Hawaii charges parking fees unless you hold its separate exemption permit. [5][10]

Does a disabled veteran plate let me park in accessible spaces?

Not always. Oregon says Disabled Veteran plates do not qualify for disabled person parking, and Kansas puts the wheelchair emblem only on plates for veterans who also meet its disability parking rules. [3][11]

How long does a temporary placard last?

Under the federal model, as long as the certifying physician sets, up to six months. California issues them for up to 180 days. [1][2]

Can someone else use my placard?

Not on their own. California says you are the only person who can use your placard or plates, and Nevada says it is illegal to park in an accessible space with them unless the person they were issued to is there. [2][4]
