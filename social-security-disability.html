SSDI or SSI: which one fits

Social Security pays disability benefits through two programs: Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) and Supplemental Security Income (SSI). [1]

SSDI: based on your work

Social Security Disability Insurance

You earn credits by working and paying Social Security taxes, and Social Security cannot pay SSDI without enough credits. Some family members, such as a child under 18 or a spouse caring for your child under 16, may also get benefits on your record.

Disability Benefits booklet (PDF) (new tab)

Sources: [1]

SSI: based on income and savings

Supplemental Security Income

Monthly payments to adults and children with income and resources below set limits who are 65 or older, blind, or have a disability. A child with a disability whose parents have low income and resources may qualify.

SSI booklet (PDF) (new tab)

Sources: [2]

  • For SSI, your resources must generally be worth $2,000 or less, or $3,000 or less for a couple. The home and land where you live do not count, and usually your car does not either. [2]
  • The basic federal SSI payment is $994 a month for one person and $1,491 for a couple in 2026, rising to $1,029 and $1,544 in 2027. The basic amount is the same nationwide; ask Social Security about your state’s amount. [3][2]

How Social Security decides who is disabled

Social Security pays only for long-term, total disability: you cannot work because of a medical condition expected to last at least one year or result in death. It does not pay for partial or short-term disability. [1]

The five questions, in order

  1. Are you working? Earnings above the monthly limit generally mean you are not considered disabled. [1][4]
  2. Is your medical condition severe? [1]
  3. Does your condition meet or medically equal one on Social Security’s list of impairments? [1]
  4. Can you do the work you did before? [1]
  5. Can you do any other type of work? [1]
  • The monthly earnings limit, called substantial gainful activity (SGA), is $1,690 in 2026 and $1,770 in 2027. For people who are statutorily blind it is $2,830 in 2026 and $2,950 in 2027. [4]
  • Some conditions, mainly certain cancers, adult brain disorders and a number of rare disorders, are on Social Security’s Compassionate Allowances list, which lets it find and decide those claims quickly. The same rules apply for SSDI and SSI. [5]

How to apply

Apply as soon as you have a disability that keeps you from working. [1]

From application to decision

  1. Gather what you can: your birth date and place, Social Security number, bank details for direct deposit, the names and contact details of your doctors and the medicines you take, and your work and earnings history. Social Security’s Adult Disability Checklist lists the rest. [6]
  2. Apply online, or call Social Security to set up an appointment by phone or at your local office. [1][6]
  3. Social Security confirms your application, online or by mail, and contacts you if it needs more information. [6]
  4. A state agency, Disability Determination Services, makes the first disability decision. [1]
  5. Social Security mails you its decision. [6]
  • You can use the online application if you are 18 or older, cannot work because of a medical condition expected to last at least 12 months or result in death, do not already get benefits on your own record, and were not denied in the last 60 days. [6]
  • Processing takes six to eight months on average. Sending the medical records and work history Social Security asks for may speed it up. [1]

After you are approved

Some benefits start right away and some take time.

  • SSDI generally has a five-month waiting period. [1]
  • You get Medicare after you have received SSDI for two years. [1]
  • If you get SSI, you may also get Medicaid, which helps pay doctor and hospital bills, and you may be able to get SNAP to help buy food. [2]

Your state’s Medicaid agency and free benefits counseling are on your state’s page and under benefits and health coverage in the directory.

If Social Security says no

Ask for reconsideration within 60 days. [7]

  • You can start the request online or fill out and upload form SSA-561. [7]
  • The appeal steps are reconsideration, a hearing, review of the hearing decision, and review by a federal district court. [7]

Quick answers

What is the difference between SSDI and SSI?

SSDI depends on your work record: you need enough work credits from paying Social Security taxes. SSI pays adults and children with low income and resources who are 65 or older, blind, or have a disability. [1][2]

How long does it take to get approved for disability?

Social Security says processing takes six to eight months on average. Sending the medical records and work history it asks for may speed it up. [1]

How much can I earn and still qualify for disability?

Earnings above a monthly limit, called substantial gainful activity, generally mean you are not considered disabled. The limit is $1,690 a month in 2026 and $1,770 in 2027, or $2,830 and $2,950 for people who are statutorily blind. [4]

How much does SSI pay?

The basic federal SSI payment is $994 a month for one person and $1,491 for a couple in 2026, and $1,029 and $1,544 in 2027. [3]

What can I do if my disability claim is denied?

Ask for reconsideration within 60 days, online or with form SSA-561. If that is denied too, the next steps are a hearing, review of the hearing decision, and federal district court. [7]

When does Medicare start for people on SSDI?

After you have received disability benefits for two years. [1]
