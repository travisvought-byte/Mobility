Treated unfairly?
Know your rights.

If an employer, landlord, school, hospital, airline or agency treated you unfairly because of a disability, find what happened to you below. Each situation says which office handles it, what to do first and, where the source states one, how long you have.

Before you start

  • Write down what happened, where, when and who was involved while you remember it.
  • Keep copies of letters, emails, notices and photos.
  • Deadlines can be short. Where a source states one, it is shown here.
  • Your state Protection and Advocacy agency helps with disability rights problems. Find yours.

At work or applying for a job

An employer treats you unfairly because of a disability, or will not make a change you need to do the job.

Who handles it
The U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) takes job discrimination charges. The Job Accommodation Network (JAN) gives free, confidential advice on accommodations.
First steps
  • Call or email JAN to plan what to ask for, and put your request in writing.
  • If you were treated unfairly, call the EEOC or use its Public Portal to start a charge. Write down dates, names and what was said.
Deadline
The EEOC says you generally must file within 180 calendar days of the discrimination, or 300 days where a state or local agency enforces a law against the same discrimination. Federal employees and applicants generally must contact their agency’s EEO counselor within 45 days.
Who to contact

A business, public place or government service

A store, restaurant, hotel, medical office, city or county office, or other public place turns you away, will not let your service animal in, or has barriers that keep you out.

Who handles it
The U.S. Department of Justice, under the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA).
First steps
  • Call the ADA Information Line and ask whether the ADA covers what happened and which part applies.
  • File a complaint on ADA.gov. Describe what happened, where and when, and what you asked for. Keep copies of letters, photos and names.
  • Check your own state’s equal-access law with the next-step finder on this site.
Who to contact
  • ADA Information Line (U.S. Department of Justice): questions about disability rights law
    Call 800-514-0301 · Official page (new tab) TTY 833-610-1264; weekday hours are on ADA.gov
  • File a disability rights complaint with the Department of Justice (ADA)
    Call 800-514-0301 · Official page (new tab) ADA Information Line; TTY 833-610-1264
  • Service animals and the ADA: what businesses and governments may ask
    Call 800-514-0301 · Official page (new tab) ADA Information Line; TTY 833-610-1264
On this site
  • Your state’s equal-access law and complaint routes
  • Your regional ADA Center and Protection and Advocacy agency
  • Service animals: what staff may ask and when a dog can be asked to leave

A complaint is not the same as a lawsuit and does not guarantee an investigation or a remedy. Your state Protection and Advocacy agency can help you decide how to proceed.

Housing

A landlord or housing provider refuses a reasonable accommodation or modification, such as a ramp, a service animal or an accessible parking space, or acts against you because you filed a complaint.

Who handles it
The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD) takes housing discrimination complaints.
First steps
  • Write down what you asked for and how the provider answered, and keep copies.
  • Say how the change you need connects to your disability.
  • Report it on the HUD page, call the HUD hotline or contact a local fair housing center.
Deadline
Complaints to HUD are generally filed within one year of the discrimination. Confirm the deadline with HUD.
Who to contact
  • HUD fair housing complaint and hotline: denied accommodation or modification (ramps, rails, accessible parking)
    Call 800-669-9777 · Official page (new tab) HUD National Discrimination Hotline, English and Spanish; Federal Relay 800-877-8339 (TTY 800-927-9275 is also published)

A baby or toddler who may need help

You are worried about how your baby or toddler is developing, or you asked for help and were told to wait and see.

Who handles it
Every state and territory has an early intervention program under Part C of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA). It serves children from birth to age three.
First steps
  • Call your state’s early intervention program and ask for an evaluation under Part C of IDEA. Parents do not have to wait for a referral.
  • The evaluation is free. Write down the name of each person you speak with and the date.
  • If your child is three or older, ask your school district for a special education evaluation instead.
  • A Parent Center can explain the steps and help if you disagree with a decision.
Who to contact
  • Early intervention for babies and toddlers: how to ask for a free evaluation
    Directory listing (new tab) Use the state list to find your program’s referral number.
  • Parent Centers: help for families of children and youth with disabilities
    Call 973-642-8100 · Official page (new tab) · Email National office of the Center for Parent Information and Resources, not a state center
On this site
  • The early intervention program in your state

For an eligible child, the steps from referral to a written service plan should take 45 days.

Source pages checked 2026-11-17: parentcenterhub.org/ei-overview · ectacenter.org/contact/ptccoord.asp · parentcenterhub.org/find-your-center

School or college

A school or college will not provide the services or accommodations a student needs, or treats a student differently because of a disability.

Who handles it
The U.S. Department of Education’s Office for Civil Rights investigates disability discrimination complaints. Parent Centers help families of children and youth with disabilities, from birth to age 26, work with schools.
First steps
  • Contact the Parent Center for your state and ask for help with the school plan and with what to do when you disagree with the school.
  • To file a discrimination complaint, use the Office for Civil Rights electronic complaint form.
Deadline
The Department’s complaint page does not state the deadline. File soon, and ask the office or a Parent Center how long you have.
Who to contact
  • Parent Centers: help for families of children and youth with disabilities
    Call 973-642-8100 · Official page (new tab) · Email National office of the Center for Parent Information and Resources, not a state center
  • School or college discrimination: file a complaint with the Office for Civil Rights
    Official page (new tab)

Health care and social services

A hospital, clinic, Medicaid office or social services agency treats you differently or shuts you out because of a disability.

Who handles it
The Office for Civil Rights at the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS). It covers programs that HHS runs or funds, and state and local government health care and social services agencies.
First steps
  • File a complaint through the OCR Complaint Portal. You can file for yourself or for someone else.
  • Write down what happened, when and where, and who was involved.
Deadline
The complaint page does not state the deadline. File as soon as you can.
Who to contact
  • Health care or social services discrimination: file a complaint with the HHS Office for Civil Rights
    Official page (new tab)

Buses, trains and paratransit

You cannot use the bus or train because of a disability, or you were denied paratransit or keep having problems with it.

Who handles it
Your local transit agency first. The Federal Transit Administration’s Office of Civil Rights takes ADA complaints about public transit.
First steps
  • Call your local transit agency and ask for its ADA paratransit application.
  • If you are denied or keep having problems, call the FTA Office of Civil Rights.
  • For Amtrak, ask for the change you need when you book, at least 72 hours ahead when you can.
Who to contact

    Call 1-800-872-7245 · Official page (new tab) 1-800-USA-RAIL. TTY 1-800-523-6590, or dial 711. Text the same number 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. Eastern

Air travel

An airline damages or loses your wheelchair, will not help you board, or refuses a service you need.

Who handles it
The airline’s Complaint Resolution Official first, then the U.S. Department of Transportation.
First steps
  • At the airport, ask for the airline’s Complaint Resolution Official.
  • If the problem is not fixed, call the DOT hotline for air travelers with disabilities.
  • After the trip, file a written or online complaint with DOT. Keep photos, repair estimates and receipts.
Who to contact
  • DOT disability hotline for air travelers: wheelchair damage, boarding and other airline problems
    Call 800-778-4838 · Official page (new tab) Weekdays 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Eastern; if you are deaf, hard of hearing or have a speech disability, dial 7-1-1 for relay
  • TSA Cares: airport security screening help for travelers with disabilities
    Call 855-787-2227 · Government page (new tab) Listed in the DOT ABCs of Accessible Travel brochure

Phones, relay and captioning

You cannot use a phone, relay, captioning or other communication service because of a disability.

Who handles it
The Federal Communications Commission’s Disability Rights Office.
First steps
  • To make a relay call, dial 711 from any phone in the United States.
  • For a problem with a service, call the FCC Disability Rights Office by voice, or its ASL Consumer Support Line by videophone, and describe what happened.
Who to contact
  • 711 relay: phone calls for people who are deaf, hard of hearing or have a speech disability
    Call 711 · Official page (new tab)

Voting

Your voting location is not accessible, there is no accessible voting device, or you are not allowed to bring someone to help you.

Who handles it
Your state or local election office first. You can report disability discrimination in voting to the Department of Justice.
First steps
  • Before Election Day, contact your election office and say exactly what you need.
  • If your voting location is not accessible, ask about curbside voting, another location or voting by mail.
  • If you are turned away, call your state Protection and Advocacy agency.
Who to contact
  • Voting with a disability: your rights and who to call
    Call 866-747-1471 · Official page (new tab) U.S. Election Assistance Commission

Social Security, Medicaid or Medicare said no

A disability claim, a service or a piece of equipment was denied, cut or delayed.

Who handles it
The agency that made the decision. The notice it sent says how to appeal.
First steps
  • Find the date on the notice and how it says to appeal.
  • Social Security: ask for reconsideration online, by form or by phone.
  • Medicaid: call your state Medicaid agency and ask how to appeal and by what date.
  • Medicare: call your State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP) or the Medicare Rights Center for help with a denial.
Deadline
Social Security says to request reconsideration within 60 days of receiving the decision. Other programs set their own deadlines; look on your notice or ask.
Who to contact

  • Medicare Rights Center helpline: denials and suppliers who will not repair or replace equipment
    Call 800-333-4114 · Official page (new tab) National consumer helpline

Abuse, neglect or exploitation

Someone is hurting, neglecting or taking money from an adult with a disability.

Who handles it
Adult Protective Services in the state where the person lives. For people in nursing homes and other care facilities, the long-term care ombudsman also takes complaints.
First steps
  • In immediate danger, call 911.
  • Report it to Adult Protective Services. Say who is at risk, where they are and what you have seen or been told. You can report without giving your name.
  • For abuse by a partner, the National Domestic Violence Hotline answers any time, and The Deaf Hotline answers in ASL.
Who to contact
  • Adult Protective Services: report abuse, neglect or exploitation of an adult with a disability
    Call 1-800-677-1116 · Directory listing (new tab) Eldercare Locator, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 9 p.m. Eastern

    Call 1-800-799-7233 · Official page (new tab) 1-800-799-SAFE. Text START to 88788
  • The Deaf Hotline: 24-hour ASL help for relationship abuse
    Call 855-812-1001 · Official page (new tab) ASL videophone line

A vocational rehabilitation or disability agency turned you down

Your state vocational rehabilitation agency or another disability program denies, delays or cuts services.

Who handles it
Every state and territory has a Protection and Advocacy agency and a Client Assistance Program. If you get SSI or SSDI and the problem is about work, the PABSS program gives free legal and advocacy help.
First steps
  • Call your state Protection and Advocacy agency, describe the decision and ask about the Client Assistance Program.
  • If you receive SSI or SSDI, say so and ask about PABSS.
Who to contact
  • PABSS: free legal and advocacy help for Social Security disability beneficiaries who work
    Call 866-968-7842 · Official page (new tab) · Email Ticket to Work Help Line, Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Eastern; press 2 for Spanish; TTY 866-833-2967
On this site
  • Your state Protection and Advocacy agency

Source pages checked 2026-11-17: choosework.ssa.gov/about/meet-your-employment-team/index.html

Help with any of these

You do not have to work this out alone.

Who handles it
Your state Protection and Advocacy agency gives information, advocacy and legal help on disability rights. Legal aid offices help people with low income with civil legal problems. Centers for Independent Living are run by disabled people and can help you make a plan.
First steps
  • Start with your state Protection and Advocacy agency.
  • For an eviction, a benefits problem or family safety, look for a legal aid office.
  • Not sure which office fits? The federal DIAL database lists disability organizations in every state and territory.
Who to contact
  • Civil legal aid for people with low income: find an office near you
    Official page (new tab)
  • DIAL: federal database of local disability organizations in every state and territory
    Official page (new tab)
