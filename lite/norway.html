Disability help in Norway

Emergency in Norway: call 113 (Ambulance and medical emergencies. Police: 112. Fire: 110).

Disability payments and benefits

Disability benefit (uføretrygd)

Nav

For people aged 18 to 67 whose earning capacity is reduced, usually by at least 50%, mainly because of long-term illness or injury, and who were members of the national insurance scheme for the five years before. Lower thresholds apply in some cases, such as an approved work injury.

Next step: Your local Nav office first assesses your ability to work with you; then you apply online on nav.no. Processing takes about six months. In Norwegian.

Equipment, home changes and getting around

Mobility aids from Nav (hjelpemidler)

Nav and its assistive technology centres

Manual and electric wheelchairs, scooters, rollators, standing frames, bed and transfer equipment and car grants (bilstønad) for people with a lasting (over two years) and significant loss of motor function, when the aid is needed for daily life, care at home, school or work.

Next step: Check your rights and find the forms on nav.no, or contact your regional assistive technology centre (hjelpemiddelsentral). Nav’s phone line is open weekdays, 9:00 to 11:15 and 12:15 to 14:30. In Norwegian.

Call 55 55 11 11nav.no

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

Equality and Anti-Discrimination Ombud (LDO)

Likestillings- og diskrimineringsombudet

Free guidance for anyone who thinks they have been discriminated against, including because of a disability. Employers and organisations can ask too.

Next step: Call the advice line Monday to Thursday, 12:30 to 14:30, or Friday, 9:00 to 11:00, or write through its secure contact forms. Its site has a page in English.

Call 23 15 73 00ldo.no

Crisis and someone to talk to

Mental Helse Helpline (Hjelpetelefonen)

Mental Helse

A listening line for anyone who needs someone to talk to, open 24 hours a day. Its web chat is open around the clock too.

Next step: Call 116 123. In Norwegian or English.
