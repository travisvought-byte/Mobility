Disability help in Cameroon

Emergency in Cameroon: call 112 (Ambulance. Police: 117; fire: 119).

Where to start

National disability card (carte nationale d'invalidité)

Ministry of Social Affairs (Minas)

An official card for people with a permanent incapacity rate of 50% or more, issued by the minister in charge of persons with disabilities. Since September 2026 the card, together with registration in the national register of persons with disabilities, gives a 50% cut in fees at public vocational training centres.

Next step: Ask the Ministry of Social Affairs (Minas) or its local social affairs office how to be assessed and apply for the card. For the training fee cut, ask the head of the training centre once you are admitted.

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

Commission des Droits de l'Homme du Cameroun (Cameroon Human Rights Commission)

Cameroon Human Rights Commission

Cameroon's institution for the promotion and protection of human rights and the prevention of torture, created by law in 2019; it refers cases brought before it to the justice authorities.

Next step: Contact the Commission in Yaoundé by phone or email.

Crisis and someone to talk to

116 children's line

Ministry of Women's Empowerment and the Family (Minproff)

A free line set up by the Ministry of Women's Empowerment and the Family to report violence, abuse, exploitation, abandonment or forced marriage of a child. Its team includes social workers, lawyers and psychologists. It answers on working days from 7:30 a.m. to 6 p.m. It is a children's line, not a general mental health line.

Next step: Call 116 on a working day between 7:30 a.m. and 6 p.m. In an emergency, call 112 for an ambulance or 117 for the police.
