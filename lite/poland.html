Disability help in Poland

Emergency in Poland: call 112 (Emergency number).

Where to start

Disability degree certificate (orzeczenie o stopniu niepełnosprawności)

District disability assessment team (powiatowy zespół do spraw orzekania o niepełnosprawności)

Formal confirmation of disability for people over 16, at a mild, moderate or significant degree. It is the key to many kinds of help, such as care benefits, a parking card, rehabilitation funding, extra leave and transport discounts, though most have extra conditions of their own. The service is free.

Next step: Have your doctor fill in the medical certificate, then take the application and your medical records to the team for where you live. You will be invited to an assessment, and the decision usually comes within 14 days of it. You can appeal within 14 days. In Polish.

Equipment, home changes and getting around

PFRON funding for orthopaedic items, aids and rehabilitation equipment

PFRON, through district family support centres (PCPR)

Helps pay for orthopaedic items, aids and rehabilitation equipment, on top of the National Health Fund (NFZ) refund, for people with a disability certificate. Household income must be below set limits: 50% of the average wage per person, or 65% for someone living alone.

Next step: Apply to your district family support centre (PCPR) with a prescription, a price quote showing the NFZ share and proof of income. In Warsaw, apply to the city’s centre for disabled people. In Polish.

Parking card (karta parkingowa)

Lets you use disabled parking spaces in Poland and abroad. Your disability certificate must include the right to a parking card; with a moderate degree, it must carry code 04-O, 05-R or 10-N. The card costs 21 zł and lasts as long as your certificate, up to five years.

Next step: Apply in person to your district or city disability assessment team with your final certificate, a 35 × 45 mm photo and proof of payment. In Polish.

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

Commissioner for Human Rights (Rzecznik Praw Obywatelskich)

Biuro Rzecznika Praw Obywatelskich

Checks whether public institutions respect your rights, and handles complaints about unequal treatment, including on grounds of disability. It also monitors how Poland applies the UN disability convention. Complaints are free.

Next step: Call the free citizens’ helpline from any phone in Poland, use the online complaint form, or email biurorzecznika@brpo.gov.pl. In Polish.

Crisis and someone to talk to

Support centre for adults in mental crisis (Centrum Wsparcia)

Fundacja Itaka

A free line, open 24 hours, for adults in a mental or emotional crisis.

Next step: Call 800 70 2222. You can also call 116 123, the national advice line for people in emotional crisis, also open 24 hours. Both are in Polish.
