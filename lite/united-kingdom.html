Disability help in the United Kingdom

Emergency in the United Kingdom: call 999 (Police, fire and ambulance).

Calling without your voice? Deaf, hard of hearing or speech disability: with Relay UK, dial 18001 and then the number you need.

Disability payments and benefits

Personal Independence Payment (PIP)

Department for Work and Pensions

A tax-free benefit for people aged 16 or over who have not reached State Pension age, to help with the extra costs of long-term ill health or disability. Older people claim Attendance Allowance, and children under 16 get Disability Living Allowance.

Next step: Start a claim by phone, Monday to Friday, 8am to 5pm, or online in some areas. Relay UK: 18001 then 0800 917 2222. From abroad: +44 191 218 7766. In Scotland, apply for Adult Disability Payment instead; Northern Ireland has its own claim line on nidirect.

Adult Disability Payment (Scotland)

Social Security Scotland

Scotland's benefit for disabled working-age adults, in place of PIP, to help with the extra costs of a disability or long-term health condition.

Next step: Apply online at mygov.scot, or call free to apply by phone and paper form. British Sign Language users can call through Contact Scotland BSL.

Call 0800 182 2222mygov.scot

Equipment, home changes and getting around

Disabled Facilities Grant

Your local council

A council grant toward changes to your home so you can keep living there. Up to £30,000 in England, £36,000 in Wales and £25,000 in Northern Ireland. Not available in Scotland. It usually depends on household income and savings, but disabled children under 18 can get one without their parents' income being counted.

Next step: Apply to your council. Do not start the work before the council approves your application, or you might not get any grant.

Blue Badge parking

Your local council

Parking help for disabled people with severe walking difficulties who travel as drivers or passengers. A badge costs up to £10 in England and £20 in Scotland, is free in Wales, and usually lasts up to 3 years.

Next step: Apply online; your council decides, usually within 12 weeks. Have a recent digital photo, proof of identity and address, and proof of any benefits ready. Northern Ireland has its own way to apply on nidirect.

Work

Access to Work

Department for Work and Pensions

Help to get or stay in work if you have a physical or mental health condition or disability. It can pay for specialist equipment, support workers, travel to work, vehicle adaptations, workplace changes, mental health support and communication support at job interviews.

Next step: Check eligibility and apply on GOV.UK.

Rights, discrimination and advocacy

Equality Advisory Support Service (EASS)

Equality Advisory Support Service

A free helpline on discrimination and your rights under the Equality Act 2010, for England, Scotland and Wales. It can explain the law, help you try to resolve the problem informally, and tell you about your options.

Next step: Call Monday to Friday 9am to 7pm, or Saturday 10am to 2pm. Textphone: 0808 800 0084. There is also a contact form and BSL interpretation on the EASS website.

Crisis and someone to talk to

Samaritans

Someone to talk to, free, any time of day or night, from any phone. You do not have to be suicidal to call.

Next step: Call 116 123.
