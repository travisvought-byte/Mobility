# Mobility Talk

mobilitytalk.org: a mobility resources and advocacy directory and guide.

A free, source-linked site for people with disabilities and the people who help them, national in the United States and now adding other countries. It began with mobility barriers (wheelchair and equipment repair, coverage denials, home access, vehicles, transit and public access) and now covers disability broadly: benefits and health coverage, housing, rides, in-home help, work and school, vision and hearing services, rights and crisis lines. It lists programs for all 50 states and D.C., with a first set of listings for the five U.S. territories. It also helps people ask lawmakers for change.

Created and maintained by Travis Vought. Corrections: travis@vethomeguard.org.

## Kinds of help and who a program is for

`scripts/extract_local_programs.py` assigns each record one of seventeen kinds of help (`categories` in the data file) and a list of `audience` tags (`audiences`). Six kinds were added in November 2026: crisis, safety and mental health; income, benefits and health coverage; in-home help, food and daily living; housing; work, school and training; vision, hearing and communication. A seventeenth, babies, children and school, was added the same month for early intervention and Parent Centers. Existing families are moved by rule in that script (blind agencies, talking-book libraries, relay and phone-equipment programs to vision and hearing; vocational rehabilitation to work), so a re-extract keeps them there.

Audience tags come from the program family and the program name, or from an explicit `audience` list on a record in `data/added-programs.json`. A record with no tag serves people with any disability. Three optional fields on added records shape the lists: `sort` pins a record to the top of its group (988 leads the crisis list), `hide_if_state_has` lets a national pointer step aside once the chosen state's own office is listed, and `link_kind` overrides the label on the main link.

`data/category-guides.json` holds, for each kind of help, the button label, a one-line description and the "Before you call" lists (what to have ready, what to ask). The directory page and the state pages both read it. They are general reminders written for this site, not rules of any program.

Territories (`TERRITORIES` in `scripts/build_mobility_routes.py`) appear in the directory and have their own pages. The law finder, the letter builder and the printed help list cover the 50 states and D.C.

`docs/GAP-AUDIT-2026-11-15.md` records the review that led to these changes and lists what is still missing.

## Crisis and shelter help

Crisis help sits where it fits and is not repeated on every page (owner direction, November 17, 2026). The urgent strip, `URGENT` in `scripts/site_parts.py`, leads every page that lists programs: 988 by call or text, how to reach it by videophone or relay, 911, and a link to the crisis lines. The home page's strip is rewritten from the shared one on every build. Shelter help is in the directory under housing (211 and the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans) and in the Spanish page's shelter section, which lists the pages written in Spanish that send readers to those lines. `tests/crisis-help.cjs` checks all of it.

## Partner page

`partner.html` lets visitors share the directory, print a handout, sponsor it at three flat yearly levels, give, or tell us how they help. There are no commissions: nobody is paid a percentage of gifts or sponsorships, and people who introduce a sponsor are thanked by name instead. Inquiries go by email; `assets/partner.js` only builds copyable links in the page and stores nothing. The give button is a PayPal donate link to Veteran Home Guardians, built in `assets/partner.js` so the account address is not written in the page source; replace it with a PayPal hosted button ID if one is created. Gifts and sponsorships are described as received by Veteran Home Guardians. `handout.html?state=XX` (script `assets/handout.js`) builds a printable help list for any state from the statewide records that have a phone number, plus national hotlines.

## State pages

`scripts/build_state_pages.py` writes `states/<state>.html` for all 50 states, D.C. and the five territories, plus `states/index.html`. Each page lists every non-veterans program for that state as plain HTML (statewide programs as cards, county programs as a compact list), so search engines and people without JavaScript can read them. The pages are generated from `data/local-programs.json`; do not edit them by hand. `npm run build` regenerates them and adds them to `sitemap.xml`. `tests/states.cjs` checks every record is present, no other state's records leak in, and links and metadata are valid. `tests/front-door.cjs` checks the home page need grid, the rights page against the directory records, the about page and the sourcing of the November 2026 records; `tests/front-door-browser.cjs` checks widths down to 320 px, focus, the no-JavaScript paths and that nothing is stored.

## Advertising

The site may carry ads, kept apart from nonprofit sponsorship (owner direction, November 19, 2026). `partner.html#advertising` states who can advertise and the ground rules; `privacy.html#ads` says how ad networks may use cookies. The "No paid placement" and "No commissions" promises stand.

Ads live in `data/ads.json`, which is empty by default; with no active ad, no page carries any ad markup. `scripts/ads.py` holds the rules and `scripts/finalize_pages.py` applies them: at most one ad on the home page and on each topic guide and state page (slots `home`, a topic page name, `state-<slug>`, `every-topic`, `every-state`), placed after the main content and never inside a list, card or help section, labeled "Advertisement", with `rel="sponsored noopener"`, and styled by `assets/ads.css`, which loads only when an ad shows. The directory, the printable help list, the Spanish page, the 404, about, partner and privacy pages never take an ad, and an ad aimed at any other slot stops the build. Start and end dates take effect when the pages are rebuilt, so rebuild after an ad starts or ends (the CI check flags pages that are out of date). `tests/ads.cjs` checks the privacy page, the partner section, that the empty file leaves no markup, and runs fixture ads through the builder without touching the pages.

## Help map

The home page and the local help page have a real map (November 21, 2026), built in the style of Nina's Map: real state and county outlines over OpenStreetMap's map pictures, with the same places listed beside the map. `assets/help-map.js` draws it with Leaflet 1.9.4, which is served from this site (`assets/vendor/`, BSD license in `assets/vendor/LICENSE-leaflet.txt`); nothing loads from a code CDN.

- Local help: with no state chosen, the map shows every state, D.C. and territory and the list beside it gives each one's count of listed programs. With a state chosen, each county is shaded by how many local programs list it (the same five steps as before), the chosen county is outlined and zoomed to, and the list shows the counties in view with their counts. Choosing on the map or in the list sets the state and County menus, and the menus move the map. "Use my location" finds the state and county inside the page (point in polygon on the outlines) and sets both menus; the location is never stored or sent.
- Home page: the map sits beside the grid of state squares. Choosing a state opens its local help; "Use my location" opens local help for your state and county.
- Accessibility: the list beside the map holds a 52-pixel button for every state or county on it, with its count, so the map never has to be used with a pointer; the map region is labeled; the page does not zoom on scroll until the map is clicked or focused; zoom buttons are 40 pixels; outlines and the list have light and dark colors (the map pictures are darkened in dark mode); no animation when reduced motion is asked for.
- Without JavaScript, or where the map cannot draw, the map section stays hidden and the state squares, the menus and the plain state pages work as before.
- Outlines: `data/geo/states.json` and `data/geo/<ST>.json`, written by `scripts/build_geo.py` from the us-atlas 3.0.1 package (U.S. Census Bureau cartographic boundaries; see the script for how to fetch it). Independent cities in Maryland, Missouri and Virginia are named "… City", as the listings name them. They are boundaries only: nothing on the map is a place or an address, and no program is pinned to a point.
- The map pictures come from tile.openstreetmap.org, which sees visitors' IP addresses and the area they view; the privacy page (`#map`), the about page and a line under each map say so.

`tests/help-map.cjs` checks the outlines, that every county a listing names can be shaded, the Leaflet files, that OpenStreetMap is the only outside source and is disclosed, and the fallbacks; `tests/help-map-browser.cjs` checks the map, the list, the menus, keyboard use, "Use my location" (in Ohio, abroad and refused), 320-pixel phones in light and dark mode, reduced motion and the no-JavaScript paths.

## Visual layer

A visual pass (November 20, 2026) made the pages friendlier without changing any fact on them:

- Small line icons, one per kind of help, drawn as inline SVG (`ICON_PATHS` and `icon()` in `scripts/site_parts.py`; no icon font or outside file). They sit on the home page's need grid (added by `scripts/finalize_pages.py`) and on each state page's group headings. Every icon is hidden from screen readers and sits beside words that say the same thing.
- State pages open with one tile per group of programs: its icon, its name and how many listings the group shows below ("12 listed"), counted from the page itself.

- Hover lifts and smooth scrolling stop when the visitor asks for reduced motion.
