# Mobility Talk

mobilitytalk.org: a mobility resources and advocacy directory and guide.

A free, source-linked site for people with disabilities and the people who help them, national in the United States and now adding other countries. It began with mobility barriers (wheelchair and equipment repair, coverage denials, home access, vehicles, transit and public access) and now covers disability broadly: benefits and health coverage, housing, rides, in-home help, work and school, vision and hearing services, rights and crisis lines. It lists programs for all 50 states and D.C., with a first set of listings for the five U.S. territories. It also helps people ask lawmakers for change.

Created and maintained by Travis Vought. Corrections: travis@vethomeguard.org.

## Kinds of help and who a program is for

`scripts/extract_local_programs.py` assigns each record one of seventeen kinds of help (`categories` in the data file) and a list of `audience` tags (`audiences`). Six kinds were added in November 2026: crisis, safety and mental health; income, benefits and health coverage; in-home help, food and daily living; housing; work, school and training; vision, hearing and communication. A seventeenth, babies, children and school, was added the same month for early intervention and Parent Centers. Existing families are moved by rule in that script (blind agencies, talking-book libraries, relay and phone-equipment programs to vision and hearing; vocational rehabilitation to work), so a re-extract keeps them there.

Audience tags come from the program family and the program name, or from an explicit `audience` list on a record in `data/added-programs.json`. A record with no tag serves people with any disability. Three optional fields on added records shape the lists: `sort` pins a record to the top of its group (988 leads the crisis list), `hide_if_state_has` lets a national pointer step aside once the chosen state's own office is listed, and `link_kind` overrides the label on the main link.

`data/category-guides.json` holds, for each kind of help, the button label, a one-line description and the "Before you call" lists (what to have ready, what to ask). The directory page and the state pages both read it. They are general reminders written for this site, not rules of any program.

Territories (`TERRITORIES` in `scripts/build_mobility_routes.py`) appear in the directory and have their own pages. The law finder, the letter builder and the printed help list cover the 50 states and D.C.

`docs/GAP-AUDIT-2026-11-15.md` records the review that led to these changes and lists what is still missing.

## Crisis and shelter help

Crisis help sits where it fits and is not repeated on every page (owner direction, November 17, 2026). The urgent strip, `URGENT` in `scripts/site_parts.py`, is on every page that lists programs: 988 by call or text, how to reach it by videophone or relay, 911, and a link to the crisis lines. It is a small side note, not a banner at the top (owner direction, November 22, 2026, repeated November 23): `SIDE_HELP` wraps it, wide screens pin it to the right edge and narrower screens show it as one quiet block after the page's own help. Country pages and the worldwide page carry their emergency box the same way, and the Spanish page has its own side note with its crisis and shelter lines after the everyday help. The home page's strip is rewritten from the shared one on every build. Shelter help is in the directory under housing (211 and the National Call Center for Homeless Veterans) and in the Spanish page's shelter section, which lists the pages written in Spanish that send readers to those lines. `tests/crisis-help.cjs` checks all of it.

## Text-only, low-data edition and offline pack

Added November 25, 2026 (owner direction: reach underserved countries and people on slow or costly connections). `scripts/build_lite_pages.py` writes `lite/index.html` and `lite/<slug>.html` for every country in `data/world.json`: one small HTML file each (about 2 to 4 KB sent), no scripts, no stylesheet to fetch and no pictures except the 1x1 visit counter, so the pages work in Opera Mini, on basic phones and on zero-rated services such as Free Basics, which only take pages that work without JavaScript, iframes, video or large images. Each page leads with the country's emergency number, lists every listing with its Call link, and ends with a ready-made SMS and WhatsApp message carrying the emergency number, the first crisis line and the page address. Lite pages name the full country page as canonical and stay out of the sitemap, so search engines keep indexing the full pages; the full country pages and world.html link to them. `tests/lite.cjs` checks all of this.

`scripts/build_offline_pack.py` packs `lite/` into `dist/mobility-talk-lite.zim` (for Kiwix readers and offline servers such as Internet-in-a-Box) and `dist/mobility-talk-lite.zip`, with the counter image removed. `.github/workflows/offline-pack.yml` runs it whenever `lite/` changes on main and replaces both files on the `offline` release, so the download links on `lite/index.html` never change. `assets/icon-48.png` is the Kiwix library icon.

## Landing pages

Every page a visitor can land on from search speaks to the person most likely to arrive there (owner direction, November 23, 2026). ## Data pipeline

Listings are mined in repeatable batches (November 23, 2026): `pipeline/registry.json` names the source families and where to look, `scripts/mining/stage.py` normalizes and deduplicates researcher output into `pipeline/batches/<date>-<name>/candidates.csv`, `scripts/mining/promote.py` publishes the new rows, and `scripts/mining/coverage.py` writes `docs/COVERAGE.md`, the list of thin places and missing families that sets the next batch. `scripts/mining/fetch_open_data.py` stages open data (the FTA National Transit Database) for review. How to run it from cold: `docs/DATA-PIPELINE.md`.
